\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Electrical Impedance Tomography (EIT) offers a promising sensing modality for prosthetic electronic skin, enabling large-area tactile perception from few boundary electrodes. However, classifiers trained on idealised simulation data fail catastrophically when confronted with the multi-source noise encountered in physical hardware---the simulation-to-reality gap. This dissertation investigates whether a physically-motivated, four-component noise model (Gaussian measurement noise, contact impedance variation, electrode positioning bias, and quantisation) can bridge this gap through noise-augmented training.

A synthetic dataset of 25,000 EIT voltage measurements across five touch classes was generated using the EIDORS forward modelling framework, simulating a 16-electrode ionic hydrogel prosthetic skin. Four classifiers (1D-CNN, MLP, SVM, Random Forest) were evaluated under systematic noise conditions with 5-fold cross-validation.

Results demonstrate that all models collapse to chance level ($\approx$20\%) under domain mismatch, but noise-matched training recovers substantial performance: the 1D-CNN achieves 75.7\% accuracy on noisy data (+55.5 percentage points over clean-trained). The standard Gaussian-only noise assumption produces artificially inflated results ($>$99\%), confirming that multi-component modelling is essential. Ablation reveals electrode bias dominates the domain gap (99.8\% of noise energy), while contact impedance causes the fastest degradation under severity scaling. Confidence calibration analysis identifies catastrophic overconfidence (ECE = 0.796) under domain shift, highlighting the need for uncertainty quantification in safety-critical deployment. Online noise augmentation paradoxically fails on fixed-noise evaluation, yet noise that persists per sample across epochs recovers performance even when drawn as an independent realisation---revealing that domain randomisation strategies require per-device noise persistence for sensor modalities with fixed per-device characteristics.

These findings provide the first systematic evidence that multi-component noise augmentation enables meaningful sim-to-real transfer for EIT tactile classification, offering actionable guidance for both noise model design and hardware prioritisation.
