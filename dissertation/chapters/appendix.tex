\label{app:training-curves}

\chapter{Exploratory Data Analysis}
\label{app:eda}

This appendix reports the empirical evidence underpinning the preprocessing decisions documented in Section~\ref{sec:method-eda}: choice of feature scaler, decision to retain the full 208-dimensional voltage vector, and decision not to deduplicate the no-contact class. Section~\ref{app:feature-reduction} then revisits the dimensionality-reduction decision under noise, where its consequences prove far larger than the clean-data margins suggest. Except where stated, results were produced by the notebook \texttt{notebooks/eda\_analysis.ipynb}.

\section{Comparison Protocol}
\label{app:eda-protocol}

Seven candidate preprocessing pipelines were compared using a logistic-regression probe under a \texttt{RepeatedStratifiedKFold} cross-validation design ($k=5$, $n_{\text{repeats}} = 3$, fixed seed $42$), yielding 15 paired evaluations per pipeline. The probe is deliberately simple: its purpose is to provide a fast, model-agnostic surface on which to compare \emph{preprocessing} effects without confounding from architecture tuning. The leakage-safe protocol fits the scaler and any reduction transform on the training partition of each fold and applies the fitted transform to the held-out partition.

\section{Held-out Confirmation}
\label{app:eda-holdout}

The cross-validation ranking was confirmed on a stratified held-out partition disjoint from the cross-validation folds, which reproduced the ordering of Table~\ref{tab:eda-pipelines}. The \texttt{RobustScaler} + full-features pipeline is the only configuration combining the highest macro-F1 with low fold-to-fold variance, which is the basis for adopting it as the default preprocessing path for all subsequent experiments in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\section{Duplicate Audit}
\label{app:eda-duplicates}

An exact-row duplicate audit on the full 25{,}000-sample dataset identified 4{,}999 duplicates within the no-contact class (Class~0) and zero duplicates across all four contact classes. The class-0 duplicates are a physically correct consequence of the homogeneous-baseline definition: every no-contact sample produces an identical conductivity image, and---in the clean (pre-noise) representation---an identical voltage difference vector of $\mathbf{0}$. Deduplication is therefore not applied. After noise injection, the class-0 samples become distinguishable through the Gaussian noise floor of Component~1 (Section~\ref{sec:method-noise-components}), so no clustering pathology arises during training.

\section{Effect of Feature Reduction}
\label{app:feature-reduction}

Dimensionality reduction was evaluated as an alternative to the full 208-dimensional representation. Table~\ref{tab:feature-reduction} reports the best model per condition across the three feature spaces.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Best accuracy (\%) per condition across feature representations. Both reductions are fitted on clean training data.}
\label{tab:feature-reduction}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Raw (208-D)} & \textbf{PCA (7-D)} & \textbf{LDA (4-D)} \\
\midrule
Clean$\to$Clean & 98.81 (MLP) & 93.56 (MLP) & 91.43 (RF) \\
Clean$\to$Noisy & 22.57 (RF)  & 24.43 (RF)  & 20.10 (RF) \\
Noisy$\to$Noisy & 75.81 (CNN) & 38.48 (SVM) & 38.14 (RF) \\
Noisy$\to$Clean & 52.23 (CNN) & 45.42 (MLP) & 22.35 (RF) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

On clean data both reductions remain competitive---\gls{lda} retains 91.43\% with only four dimensions, confirming that the class-discriminative structure is genuinely low-dimensional. Under noise they are actively harmful, halving Noisy$\to$Noisy accuracy from 75.81\% to approximately 38\%. Because the projections are estimated from the covariance of \emph{clean} data, they retain only the directions that separate classes in the absence of noise; the dominant corruption acts along different directions (Section~\ref{sec:res-domain}), so the information a model would need in order to discount it is discarded before training begins. This is a concrete instance of the result of \textcite{ben-david2010domain} that minimising source-domain error is insufficient when the target distribution differs.
