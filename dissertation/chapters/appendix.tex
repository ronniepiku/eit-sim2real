\section{Training Convergence}
\label{app:training-curves}

Figures~\ref{fig:app-training-noisy}--\ref{fig:app-training-clean} show training and validation loss curves for the CNN under the key training regimes. All three converge stably, confirming that the accuracy differences reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reflect what each regime learns rather than any failure to optimise. The noise-trained models converge more slowly than the clean-trained model, consistent with the more complex decision boundaries required when a large structured offset must be discounted.

\chapter{Exploratory Data Analysis}
\label{app:eda}

This appendix reports the empirical evidence underpinning the preprocessing decisions documented in Section~\ref{sec:method-eda}: choice of feature scaler, decision to retain the full 208-dimensional voltage vector, and decision not to deduplicate the no-contact class. All results were produced by the notebook \texttt{notebooks/eda\_analysis.ipynb}.

\section{Comparison Protocol}
\label{app:eda-protocol}

Seven candidate preprocessing pipelines were compared using a logistic-regression probe under a \texttt{RepeatedStratifiedKFold} cross-validation design ($k=5$, $n_{\text{repeats}} = 3$, fixed seed $42$), yielding 15 paired evaluations per pipeline. The probe is deliberately simple: its purpose is to provide a fast, model-agnostic surface on which to compare \emph{preprocessing} effects without confounding from architecture tuning. The leakage-safe protocol fits the scaler and any reduction transform on the training partition of each fold and applies the fitted transform to the held-out partition.

\section{Held-out Confirmation}
\label{app:eda-holdout}

The cross-validation ranking was confirmed on a stratified held-out partition disjoint from the cross-validation folds, which reproduced the ordering of Table~\ref{tab:eda-pipelines}. The \texttt{RobustScaler} + full-features pipeline is the only configuration combining the highest macro-F1 with low fold-to-fold variance, which is the basis for adopting it as the default preprocessing path for all subsequent experiments in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\section{Duplicate Audit}
\label{app:eda-duplicates}

An exact-row duplicate audit on the full 25{,}000-sample dataset identified 4{,}999 duplicates within the no-contact class (Class~0) and zero duplicates across all four contact classes. The class-0 duplicates are a physically correct consequence of the homogeneous-baseline definition: every no-contact sample produces an identical conductivity image, and---in the clean (pre-noise) representation---an identical voltage difference vector of $\mathbf{0}$. Deduplication is therefore not applied. After noise injection, the class-0 samples become distinguishable through the Gaussian noise floor of Component~1 (Section~\ref{sec:method-noise-components}), so no clustering pathology arises during training.
