\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-background}

The sense of touch is fundamental to human interaction with the physical world. Biological skin integrates four principal mechanoreceptor types---Merkel cells, Meissner corpuscles, Ruffini endings, and Pacinian corpuscles---providing rich, distributed tactile feedback across pressure, texture, temperature, and pain \parencite{chortos2016, hammock2013}. This multimodal perception enables safe manipulation of objects, social communication through physical contact, and environmental hazard awareness \parencite{lee2020}. For the estimated 57.7 million people worldwide living with upper-limb loss \parencite{mcdonald2021}, the absence of tactile sensation in prosthetic devices represents a significant barrier to natural, intuitive use. \textcite{biddiss2007} demonstrated that lack of sensory feedback is a primary driver of prosthetic abandonment, with rejection rates exceeding 35\% for myoelectric prostheses---a finding that underscores the clinical urgency of developing functional tactile feedback systems.

Electronic skin (e-skin) technologies aim to address this deficit by developing flexible, large-area sensor systems capable of mimicking the distributed sensing of biological skin \parencite{hammock2013, chortos2016, lee2020}. A range of material platforms have been explored---including hydrogels offering biocompatibility and self-healing \parencite{yao2022, sun2019}, conductive elastomers providing piezoresistive response and durability \parencite{lee2019, park2022}, and fully-printed flexible arrays enabling manufacturing scalability \parencite{zheng2020}. Among the various sensing modalities employed in these platforms---piezoresistive, capacitive, optical, and tomographic---\gls{eit} has emerged as particularly promising for prosthetic applications \parencite{silvera-tawil2015, chen2023}.

\gls{eit} reconstructs the internal conductivity distribution of a material from boundary voltage measurements collected via a small number of electrodes arranged around the periphery of a conductive medium \parencite{cheney1999, adler2006}. When an external stimulus locally alters conductivity, these changes are reflected in the boundary voltage patterns. In ionic hydrogel substrates---the material platform adopted in this work---mechanical compression disrupts ion-transport pathways within the hydrated polymer network, producing a \emph{positive piezoresistive response}: local resistance increases (equivalently, conductivity decreases) under applied force \parencite{lee2019, yao2022}. The appeal of \gls{eit} for prosthetic skin lies in several practical advantages. First, it requires far fewer sensing elements than discrete arrays: a 16-electrode system can achieve continuous spatial sensing, whereas conventional approaches require one sensor per location \parencite{silvera-tawil2015, park2022}. Second, the absence of dense internal wiring makes \gls{eit}-based skins inherently more flexible and durable \parencite{yao2022, kim2019}. Third, ionic hydrogel substrates are naturally compliant, self-healing, and biocompatible, aligning with the requirements of wearable prosthetics \parencite{sun2019, lee2019}.

Recent machine learning advances have demonstrated that direct classification of \gls{eit} voltage vectors can achieve high accuracy for touch recognition tasks: \textcite{yao2022} reported 98.7\% on 5-class classification, \textcite{chen2023} demonstrated CNN-enhanced reconstruction validated in human interaction scenarios, and \textcite{luo2021} achieved robust localisation and force estimation using fibre Bragg gratings with deep neural networks and 5-fold cross-validation. These results suggest that ML-enabled \gls{eit} skins could provide clinically meaningful tactile feedback. However, a critical gap exists between these controlled laboratory demonstrations and the conditions encountered in real prosthetic deployment.

\section{The Simulation-to-Reality Challenge}
\label{sec:intro-sim2real}

Despite the promise of ML-enabled \gls{eit} sensing, a fundamental barrier to deployment is the \emph{simulation-to-reality gap}: models trained on computationally generated data fail to maintain performance on physical hardware. \gls{eit} training data are almost exclusively generated through forward models such as EIDORS \parencite{adler2006}, which produce idealised measurements diverging substantially from real hardware conditions \parencite{cheney1999}. Real systems are subject to at least five compounding degradation sources---Gaussian measurement noise (\gls{snr} 40--60~dB), contact impedance variation, temporal drift, electrode positioning bias, and quantisation effects \parencite{adler2006, vilhunen2002, boone1996, kolehmainen1997}---yet the noise augmentation strategies in prior \gls{eit} studies remain limited to simple additive Gaussian noise \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}.

This challenge is well-characterised in adjacent domains. \textcite{tobin2017} showed that domain randomisation during simulation enables zero-shot transfer to physical robots, while \textcite{ganin2016} and \textcite{tzeng2017adda} proposed adversarial domain adaptation for cross-domain generalisation. \textcite{hendrycks2019} demonstrated that image classifiers suffer up to 40\% accuracy degradation under common corruptions. Yet none of these techniques has been applied to \gls{eit} tactile sensing, and no prior work has investigated whether multi-component, physically-motivated noise augmentation can bridge this gap.

\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec:intro-gap}

As Chapter~\ref{ch:literature_review} demonstrates through a systematic review of the field, the existing literature reveals five interconnected gaps that collectively limit progress toward deployable \gls{eit} tactile systems: (1) simplistic single-component noise models \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}; (2) no systematic robustness evaluation under varying noise severity; (3) missing ablation of individual noise contributions; (4) inadequate validation methodology across studies \parencite{jiang2023, fang2022}; and (5) no standardised benchmarks enabling cross-study comparison. Crucially, no prior work addresses these gaps simultaneously within a unified framework.

\section{Research Aims and Objectives}
\label{sec:intro-aims}

This dissertation investigates whether incorporating a physically-motivated, multi-component noise model during the training phase of ML classifiers improves their robustness when applied to noisy \gls{eit} tactile measurements---framing noise augmentation as a form of domain randomisation \parencite{tobin2017} for simulation-to-reality transfer. Specifically, the research addresses the following objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Develop a configurable noise model} capturing five principal noise sources present in real \gls{eit} systems: Gaussian measurement noise, contact impedance variation, temporal drift, electrode positioning bias, and quantisation noise. Each component is parameterised based on values reported in the experimental \gls{eit} literature \parencite{adler2006, vilhunen2002, boone1996, kolehmainen1997}.
    
    \item \textbf{Generate a physics-informed synthetic \gls{eit} dataset} using the EIDORS forward modelling framework \parencite{adler2006}, simulating a 16-electrode ionic hydrogel prosthetic skin sensing five distinct touch classes (no contact, light touch, firm press, point contact, and distributed contact). Conductivity parameters are derived from the literature-reported positive piezoresistive response of ionic hydrogels \parencite{lee2019}, with contact geometry informed by Hertzian contact mechanics for soft elastomers \parencite{kim2019}.
    
    \item \textbf{Train and compare multiple classifiers}---including a 1D \gls{cnn}, \gls{svm} \parencite{cortes1995}, \gls{rf} \parencite{breiman2001}, and \gls{mlp}---on both clean and noise-augmented data, providing the first systematic comparison of classical and deep approaches under identical noise conditions for \gls{eit} classification.
    
    \item \textbf{Evaluate robustness} by systematically testing trained models under varying noise severity levels, following the multi-corruption, multi-severity paradigm established by \textcite{hendrycks2019}.
    
    \item \textbf{Conduct an ablation study} to identify which noise components contribute most to classifier degradation, informing priorities for noise mitigation in future hardware design and simulation fidelity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

This work makes the following contributions to the field:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A five-component, literature-justified noise model for \gls{eit} tactile simulation that captures the dominant real-world distortions more comprehensively than the single-component Gaussian noise used in prior work \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}.
    
    \item An open-source, reproducible pipeline spanning data generation (MATLAB/EIDORS), model training (Python/PyTorch), evaluation, and visualisation, facilitating future benchmarking and addressing the standardisation gap identified by \textcite{jiang2023}.
    
    \item Empirical evidence quantifying the robustness benefit of noise-augmented training for \gls{eit} touch classification, with systematic ablation across noise components---inspired by the corruption benchmark methodology of \textcite{hendrycks2019} but adapted to \gls{eit}-specific distortions.
    
    \item A comparative study of classical ML baselines (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}, \gls{mlp}) against a purpose-designed 1D-\gls{cnn} architecture under identical noise conditions, providing practical guidance on model selection for resource-constrained prosthetic systems where computational efficiency is critical \parencite{wang2020, zhang2022fista}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Dissertation Structure}
\label{sec:intro-structure}

The remainder of this dissertation is organised as follows:

\begin{description}
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:literature_review}] presents a critical review of the literature spanning electronic skin technologies, \gls{eit} sensing principles, machine learning approaches for tactile classification, simulation-to-reality transfer techniques, and deployment considerations.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}] details the research methodology, including the EIDORS simulation setup, noise model formulation with literature-derived parameters, dataset generation procedure, model architectures, training protocols, and evaluation framework.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:results}] reports the experimental findings, including classification performance, robustness evaluation under multi-severity noise, and the noise component ablation study.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}] interprets the results in the context of the research objectives and prior literature, discusses limitations, and considers implications for prosthetic skin deployment.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}] summarises the key findings, evaluates the extent to which the research objectives were met, and identifies directions for future work including temporal modelling, domain-adversarial adaptation, and real-hardware validation.
\end{description}
