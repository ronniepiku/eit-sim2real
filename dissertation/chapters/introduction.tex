\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-background}

The sense of touch is fundamental to human interaction with the physical world. Biological skin integrates four principal mechanoreceptor types---Merkel cells, Meissner corpuscles, Ruffini endings, and Pacinian corpuscles---providing distributed tactile feedback across pressure, texture, temperature, and pain \parencite{chortos2016, hammock2013}. This multimodal perception enables safe manipulation, social communication through physical contact, and environmental hazard detection \parencite{lee2020}. For the estimated 57.7 million people worldwide living with upper-limb loss \parencite{mcdonald2021}, the absence of tactile sensation in prosthetic devices represents a significant barrier to natural use. \textcite{biddiss2007} demonstrated that lack of sensory feedback is a primary driver of prosthetic abandonment, with rejection rates exceeding 35\% for myoelectric prostheses---a finding corroborated by \textcite{fang2022} in a systematic review of ML in prosthetics. This underscores the clinical urgency of developing functional tactile feedback systems.

Electronic skin (e-skin) technologies aim to address this deficit by developing flexible, large-area sensor systems capable of mimicking biological skin's distributed sensing \parencite{hammock2013, chortos2016, lee2020}. Material platforms include hydrogels offering biocompatibility and self-healing \parencite{yao2022, sun2019}, conductive elastomers providing piezoresistive response and durability \parencite{lee2019, park2022}, and fully-printed flexible arrays enabling manufacturing scalability \parencite{zheng2020}. Among sensing modalities, \gls{eit} has emerged as particularly promising for prosthetic applications due to its minimal wiring requirements and large-area coverage from few electrodes \parencite{silvera-tawil2015, chen2023}.

\gls{eit} reconstructs internal conductivity distributions from boundary voltage measurements collected via electrodes arranged around the periphery of a conductive medium \parencite{cheney1999, adler2006}. When an external stimulus locally alters conductivity, these changes manifest in the boundary voltage patterns. In ionic hydrogel substrates (the material platform adopted in this work), mechanical compression disrupts ion-transport pathways, producing a \emph{positive piezoresistive response}: conductivity decreases under applied force \parencite{lee2019, yao2022}. The appeal of \gls{eit} for prosthetic skin lies in three practical advantages: (1)~a 16-electrode system achieves continuous spatial sensing where conventional approaches require one discrete sensor per location \parencite{silvera-tawil2015, park2022}; (2)~the absence of dense internal wiring makes \gls{eit}-based skins inherently more flexible and durable \parencite{yao2022, kim2019}; and (3)~ionic hydrogel substrates are naturally compliant, self-healing, and biocompatible \parencite{sun2019, lee2019}.

Recent machine learning advances have demonstrated that direct classification of \gls{eit} voltage vectors can achieve high accuracy for touch recognition. \textcite{yao2022} reported 98.7\% on 5-class classification, \textcite{chen2023} demonstrated CNN-enhanced reconstruction validated in human interaction scenarios, and \textcite{luo2021} achieved robust localisation using fibre Bragg gratings with deep neural networks. These results suggest that ML-enabled \gls{eit} skins could provide clinically meaningful tactile feedback. However, these accuracies are achieved under idealised conditions with single-component or no noise augmentation \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}, and a critical gap exists between controlled laboratory demonstrations and the conditions encountered in real prosthetic deployment \parencite{jiang2023}.

\section{The Simulation-to-Reality Challenge}
\label{sec:intro-sim2real}

Despite this promise, a fundamental barrier to deployment is the \emph{simulation-to-reality gap}: models trained on computationally generated data fail to maintain performance on physical hardware \parencite{pan2010survey}. \gls{eit} training data are almost exclusively generated through forward models such as EIDORS \parencite{adler2006}, producing idealised measurements that diverge substantially from real hardware conditions \parencite{cheney1999}. Real systems are subject to at least four compounding degradation sources---Gaussian measurement noise (\gls{snr} 40--60~dB), contact impedance variation, electrode positioning bias, and quantisation effects \parencite{adler2006, vilhunen2002, kolehmainen1997}---yet noise augmentation strategies in prior \gls{eit} studies remain limited to simple additive Gaussian noise \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}. \textcite{ben-david2010domain} formalised theoretically that a classifier's target-domain error is bounded by the source-domain error plus the divergence between domains, establishing that reducing this divergence---through noise augmentation---directly improves generalisation guarantees.

This challenge is well-characterised in adjacent domains. \textcite{tobin2017} showed that domain randomisation during simulation enables zero-shot transfer to physical robots, while \textcite{ganin2016} and \textcite{tzeng2017adda} proposed adversarial domain adaptation for cross-domain generalisation. \textcite{hendrycks2019} demonstrated that image classifiers suffer up to 40\% accuracy degradation under common corruptions, and \textcite{rusak2020simple} showed that Gaussian noise augmentation alone is insufficient---diverse, physically-grounded augmentation is required for genuine robustness. Yet none of these techniques has been applied to \gls{eit} tactile sensing, and no prior work has investigated whether multi-component, physically-motivated noise augmentation can bridge this gap.

\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec:intro-gap}

As Chapter~\ref{ch:literature_review} demonstrates through a systematic review, the literature reveals five interconnected gaps collectively limiting progress toward deployable \gls{eit} tactile systems: (1)~simplistic single-component noise models \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}; (2)~no systematic robustness evaluation under varying noise severity \parencite{hendrycks2019}; (3)~missing ablation of individual noise contributions; (4)~inadequate validation methodology across studies \parencite{jiang2023, fang2022}; and (5)~no standardised benchmarks enabling cross-study comparison \parencite{caruana2006empirical}. Crucially, no prior work addresses these gaps simultaneously within a unified framework.

\section{Research Aims and Objectives}
\label{sec:intro-aims}

This dissertation investigates whether incorporating a physically-motivated, multi-component noise model during training improves classifier robustness when applied to noisy \gls{eit} tactile measurements, framing noise augmentation as domain randomisation \parencite{tobin2017} for simulation-to-reality transfer. The theoretical basis for this approach derives from domain adaptation theory \parencite{ben-david2010domain, pan2010survey}, which establishes that minimising the distributional divergence between training and deployment conditions directly reduces generalisation error. Specifically, the research addresses the following objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Develop a configurable noise model} capturing four principal noise sources in real \gls{eit} systems: Gaussian measurement noise, contact impedance variation, electrode positioning bias, and quantisation noise. Each component is parameterised from values reported in the experimental literature \parencite{adler2006, vilhunen2002, kolehmainen1997}, with contact impedance and bias applied per-electrode to reflect the physical sensing structure. \emph{Success criterion}: independently toggle-able, literature-justified, and producing physically plausible voltage perturbations validated by signal analysis.
    
    \item \textbf{Generate a physics-informed synthetic \gls{eit} dataset} using the EIDORS forward modelling framework \parencite{adler2006}, simulating a 16-electrode ionic hydrogel prosthetic skin sensing five touch classes (no contact, light touch, firm press, point contact, and distributed contact). Conductivity parameters derive from a pressure-based piezoresistive model consistent with the positive piezoresistive response of ionic hydrogels \parencite{lee2019}, with contact geometry informed by Hertzian mechanics \parencite{kim2019}. \emph{Success criterion}: demonstrable class separation in feature space and balanced class distribution.
    
    \item \textbf{Train and compare multiple classifiers}---a 1D \gls{cnn}, \gls{svm} \parencite{cortes1995}, \gls{rf} \parencite{breiman2001}, and \gls{mlp}---on both clean and noise-augmented data, providing the first systematic comparison of classical and deep approaches under identical noise conditions for \gls{eit} classification. \emph{Success criterion}: statistically significant performance differences established via paired testing \parencite{demsar2006statistical}.
    
    \item \textbf{Evaluate robustness} by testing trained models under varying noise severity levels, following the multi-corruption, multi-severity paradigm of \textcite{hendrycks2019}. Extended analyses including confidence calibration \parencite{guo2017calibration}, per-class robustness profiling, and domain gap quantification \parencite{gretton2012kernel} provide comprehensive robustness characterisation. \emph{Success criterion}: quantified robustness margins and graceful degradation rates demonstrating noise augmentation benefit.
    
    \item \textbf{Conduct an ablation study} to identify which noise components contribute most to classifier degradation, informing priorities for noise mitigation in future hardware design. Noise parameter sensitivity and hyperparameter sensitivity analyses verify that conclusions generalise beyond specific modelling choices. \emph{Success criterion}: rank-ordering of component importance with supporting statistical evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

This work makes the following contributions to the field:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A four-component, literature-justified noise model for \gls{eit} tactile simulation that captures dominant real-world distortions more comprehensively than the single-component Gaussian noise used in prior work \parencite{chen2023, yao2022}. Crucially, contact impedance and bias are applied per-electrode rather than per-measurement, reflecting the physical structure of the electrode--substrate interface \parencite{vilhunen2002, kolehmainen1997}.
    
    \item An open-source, reproducible pipeline spanning data generation (MATLAB/EIDORS), model training (Python/PyTorch), evaluation, and visualisation, facilitating future benchmarking and addressing the standardisation gap identified by \textcite{jiang2023}.
    
    \item Empirical evidence quantifying the robustness benefit of noise-augmented training for \gls{eit} touch classification, with systematic ablation across noise components---inspired by the corruption benchmark methodology of \textcite{hendrycks2019} but adapted to \gls{eit}-specific distortions.
    
    \item A comparative study of classical ML baselines (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}, \gls{mlp}) against a purpose-designed 1D-\gls{cnn} architecture under identical noise conditions, providing practical guidance for resource-constrained prosthetic systems \parencite{wang2020, zhang2022fista}.
    
    \item An extended evaluation framework comprising confidence calibration analysis \parencite{guo2017calibration}, per-class robustness profiling, domain gap quantification via Maximum Mean Discrepancy \parencite{gretton2012kernel}, noise parameter sensitivity analysis, and hyperparameter sensitivity verification---providing the multi-faceted robustness characterisation absent from prior \gls{eit} studies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Dissertation Structure}
\label{sec:intro-structure}

The remainder of this dissertation is organised as follows:

\begin{description}
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:literature_review}] presents a critical review of the literature spanning electronic skin technologies, \gls{eit} sensing principles, machine learning approaches for tactile classification, simulation-to-reality transfer techniques, and deployment considerations.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}] details the research methodology, including the EIDORS simulation setup, noise model formulation with literature-derived parameters, dataset generation procedure, model architectures, training protocols, and evaluation framework.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:results}] reports the experimental findings, including classification performance, robustness evaluation under multi-severity noise, and the noise component ablation study.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}] interprets the results in the context of the research objectives and prior literature, discusses limitations, and considers implications for prosthetic skin deployment.
    
    \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}] summarises the key findings, evaluates the extent to which the research objectives were met, and identifies directions for future work including temporal modelling, domain-adversarial adaptation, and real-hardware validation.
\end{description}

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:intro-scope}

The following scope constraints define the boundaries of this investigation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simulation-only.} All data are generated via forward modelling; no physical hardware validation is performed. This is deliberate: the research question (``does noise-augmented training improve robustness?'') can be rigorously answered in simulation by controlling test-time corruption, whereas hardware validation conflates noise model accuracy with classifier robustness. Hardware transfer is positioned as future work (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}).
    
    \item \textbf{Static single-frame classification.} Each sample represents an instantaneous measurement frame; temporal dynamics (sliding contact, force ramps, drift) are not modelled. This bounds the problem to the most common formulation in the \gls{eit} classification literature \parencite{yao2022, chen2023}, while temporal extensions are identified as future work.
    
    \item \textbf{2D geometry.} The forward model uses a 2D circular cross-section rather than a 3D volume. This is computationally necessary for 25{,}000 forward solves and consistent with prior \gls{eit} classification work \parencite{chen2023, lee2019}, though it limits direct applicability to cylindrical prosthetic geometries.
    
    \item \textbf{Five-class taxonomy.} The class definitions represent archetypal prosthetic interactions but do not exhaustively cover all possible tactile events (e.g., shear, vibration, thermal contact). The taxonomy is informed by \textcite{silvera-tawil2012eit} and \textcite{yao2022}.
\end{itemize}

These boundaries ensure that findings are clearly interpretable: any robustness improvement can be attributed to the noise augmentation strategy rather than confounded by hardware variability or temporal modelling.
