\section{Electronic Skin for Prosthetic Applications}
\label{sec:lit-eskin}

\subsection{Biological Motivation and Clinical Need}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-bio}

Human skin integrates four principal mechanoreceptor types---Merkel cells for sustained pressure, Meissner corpuscles for light touch, Ruffini endings for skin stretch, and Pacinian corpuscles for vibration---within a soft, self-healing, large-area substrate \parencite{chortos2016}. This multimodal, distributed sensing enables dexterous manipulation, social communication through physical contact, and environmental hazard detection \parencite{hammock2013}. \textcite{mcdonald2021} estimate 57.7 million people globally live with upper-limb loss, creating substantial clinical demand for prosthetic systems that restore even a subset of these tactile capabilities.

\textcite{biddiss2007} demonstrated that the absence of tactile feedback is a primary factor in prosthetic device abandonment, with rejection rates exceeding 35\% for myoelectric prostheses. This finding---corroborated by subsequent reviews \parencite{fang2022}---underscores the clinical urgency of functional sensory feedback. Restoring touch requires electronic skins that combine: (i) mechanical compliance and durability; (ii) multimodal sensing across pressure, temperature, and texture; (iii) high spatial resolution over large areas; and (iv) robustness to long-term drift \parencite{chortos2016, lee2020}.

\textcite{fang2022} conducted a systematic review of machine learning in prosthetics and orthotics, finding that while ML techniques show promise---SVMs achieving 96.7\% classification accuracy with 5-fold cross-validation for prosthetic socket alignment---methodological reporting remains inconsistent across studies, with many providing insufficient detail on patient demographics and dataset characteristics. This limits assessment of cross-user generalisability, a critical requirement for real-world prosthetic deployment.

\subsection{Material Platforms for Electronic Skin}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-materials}

The choice of substrate material fundamentally constrains sensing performance, durability, and wearability. Three dominant material platforms have emerged, each presenting distinct trade-offs that can be evaluated against four criteria critical for prosthetic deployment: \emph{temporal stability} (resistance to drift), \emph{mechanical durability}, \emph{sensing fidelity} (linearity, sensitivity), and \emph{manufacturing scalability}. \textbf{Hydrogels} offer the closest approximation to biological tissue in terms of compliance and biocompatibility \parencite{yao2022, sun2019}. Crucially, ionic hydrogels exhibit a \emph{positive piezoresistive response} where mechanical compression disrupts ion-transport pathways within the hydrated polymer matrix, increasing local resistance (decreasing conductivity) under applied force \parencite{lee2019}. This is the inverse of the response exhibited by carbon-black--filled elastomers, where compression increases inter-particle contact and \emph{decreases} resistance. The ionic mechanism is directly relevant to \gls{eit} sensing because the magnitude and sign of the conductivity change determine the resulting boundary voltage patterns. \textcite{yao2022} achieved 98.7\% touch classification accuracy on a hydrogel--elastomer hybrid using a CNN operating on \gls{eit} voltage difference vectors, while \textcite{sun2019} demonstrated multifunctional sensing (material and texture classification at 95--97\% accuracy). However, both works reveal the fundamental weakness of this platform. Moisture loss progressively alters baseline conductivity, introducing domain shift over time that neither study addresses through re-calibration or drift-robust training. This instability represents a deployment challenge that motivates robust training approaches. The present work adopts ionic hydrogel as the simulated substrate, modelling conductivity \emph{decreases} of 5--65\% relative to the homogeneous baseline under varying touch forces, with the magnitude determined by local mechanical pressure $P = F/(\pi r^2)$, where $F$ is the applied force and $r$ is the contact radius. This pressure-based parametrisation is informed by the piezoresistive characterisation reported by \textcite{lee2019} and the force ranges documented by \textcite{zhang2022fista} (50--200\,g on hydrogel substrates), yielding larger conductivity changes for small-area contacts (high local pressure) and smaller changes for distributed contacts (low local pressure).

\textbf{Conductive elastomers} (carbon-filled silicone) eliminate moisture dependence at the cost of nonlinear piezoresistive response. \textcite{park2022} demonstrated adaptive sensitivity via granular jamming, and \textcite{lee2019} achieved 500\% sensitivity improvement through multimaterial anisotropic conductivity with self-healing capability. Critically, \textcite{kim2019} validated stability over 10,000 cycles but documented gradual degradation from material fatigue and temperature sensitivity, confirming that even the most durable platform cannot eliminate drift entirely.

\textbf{Printed flexible electronics} \parencite{zheng2020} achieve 388.5~kPa$^{-1}$ sensitivity with manufacturing scalability, but are limited to low-resolution discrete arrays (81 taxels) incompatible with the distributed sensing paradigm of \gls{eit}.

The central insight from this comparison is that \emph{no material platform eliminates the noise and drift challenges that motivate this work}. Hydrogels drift fastest, elastomers drift slowest, but all platforms produce measurements that diverge from idealised simulation over time. This material-agnostic persistence of the simulation-to-reality gap strengthens the case for noise-robust ML rather than relying on material improvements alone. The present work's noise model captures the dominant per-frame distortions (measurement noise, contact impedance, positioning bias, and quantisation) that are common to all platforms, while temporal drift is deferred to future temporal modelling extensions.

Among these, \gls{eit} and fibre-optic approaches stand out for large-area prosthetic applications. Critically, the choice of sensing modality determines the nature of the sim-to-real challenge: piezoresistive and capacitive arrays suffer from discrete sensor failures and interconnect degradation, while \gls{eit} faces a fundamentally different problem---the inverse problem's ill-posedness means that noise corrupts not individual sensors but the \emph{entire} spatial reconstruction, propagating measurement errors non-locally across the sensing area. This distinction motivates modality-specific noise models rather than generic data augmentation. \textcite{luo2021} demonstrated Ruffini-receptor-inspired tactile sensing using fibre Bragg gratings (FBGs) with deep neural networks, employing four separate MLPs for contact localisation and a CNN for force estimation. Their methodological rigour including clear task separation, detailed architecture description, and real-time inference demonstration, sets a benchmark that many \gls{eit}-based studies have not yet matched. However, FBG systems require expensive optical interrogators, limiting accessibility. \gls{eit}, by contrast, requires only simple current injection and voltage measurement hardware, making it more suitable for cost-sensitive prosthetic applications \parencite{silvera-tawil2015}.

\textcite{jiang2023} argued that ML is essential for unlocking the full potential of advanced e-skin systems, but that progress depends on overcoming hardware constraints, improving material durability, and developing ML pipelines robust to noise, drift, and limited data. This review identifies the same challenges and notes that \gls{eit} skins face these issues acutely due to the ill-posed nature of the inverse problem. Notably, \textcite{silvera-tawil2012eit} demonstrated one of the earliest ML-based touch modality classification systems using \gls{eit}, employing a LogitBoost classifier with handcrafted features (intensity change, contact area, temporal dynamics) extracted from 224 boundary voltage measurements acquired at 60~Hz. This early work established the feasibility of direct classification from \gls{eit} measurements without intermediate reconstruction, but used classical features rather than learned representations, leaving substantial scope for deep learning approaches.

\subsection{Bio-inspired Signal Encoding and Multimodal Perception}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-bioinspired}

\textcite{osborn2016} proposed a biomimetic circuit mimicking afferent mechanoreceptor firing patterns (8--64~Hz response band), using \gls{pca}-reduced features classified by k-NN ($k$=5) and \gls{svm} (RBF kernel) across 19 touch classes with 5-fold cross-validation. Near-perfect accuracy was reported, but under highly controlled laboratory conditions with no environmental variability, suggesting limited transferability to real prosthetic use. Nevertheless, the work demonstrates that biologically-grounded signal encoding prior to classification can substantially simplify the learning problem. This principle is relevant to the present study's use of voltage difference vectors rather than raw impedance measurements.

\textcite{lee2020} reviewed the convergence of e-skins and ML for intelligent soft robots, emphasising that next-generation systems must integrate pressure, shear, strain, and temperature sensing with temporal modelling capabilities. They highlight the growing use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for sequential contact dynamics. This approach is largely absent from \gls{eit} tactile studies, where static single-frame classification dominates. This temporal dimension represents an important limitation of the present work's static classification framework, but also an opportunity for future extension.

\section{Electrical Impedance Tomography for Tactile Sensing}
\label{sec:lit-eit}

\subsection{Principles of EIT}
\label{sec:lit-eit-principles}

\gls{eit} reconstructs the internal conductivity distribution $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ of a body $\Omega$ from boundary voltage measurements. Small alternating currents are injected through electrode pairs on the boundary $\partial\Omega$, and the resulting voltage differences are measured across remaining pairs. The relationship between conductivity and boundary measurements is governed by the generalised Laplace equation:

\begin{equation}
\nabla \cdot (\sigma(\mathbf{x}) \nabla u(\mathbf{x})) = 0, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega
\label{eq:eit-forward}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $u(\mathbf{x})$ is the electric potential. The forward problem is well-posed and typically solved using the \gls{fem} \parencite{cheney1999, adler2006}. The inverse problem---recovering $\sigma$ from boundary measurements---is severely ill-posed and small measurement errors can produce large, non-physical reconstruction artefacts \parencite{cheney1999}.

\textcite{cheney1999} provided the foundational mathematical treatment of \gls{eit}, establishing that the problem's ill-posedness necessitates regularisation. They described early reconstruction methods including back-projection, one-step Newton methods (e.g., the NOSER algorithm achieving $\sim$60 reconstructions per second), and iterative Gauss--Newton approaches. These methods enforce smoothness or sparsity constraints but inherently limit spatial resolution. Crucially, Cheney et al.\ established that reconstruction quality is strongly dependent on electrode configuration, measurement noise, and model assumptions. \textcite{liu2022advances} provide a comprehensive recent survey of EIT solution methods from traditional regularisation through to modern deep learning, documenting the progressive shift from physics-based to data-driven approaches while noting that hybrid methods combining physical constraints with learned representations offer the best trade-off between stability and accuracy.

\subsection{EIT Measurement Patterns and Data Representation}
\label{sec:lit-eit-patterns}

For a system with $n_e$ electrodes, the adjacent drive pattern injects current through neighbouring electrode pairs and measures voltages across all remaining pairs, yielding $n_e(n_e-3)/2$ independent measurements per frame. A 16-electrode system thus produces 208 independent voltage measurements \parencite{adler2009}. The GREIT framework \parencite{adler2009} introduced a unified approach to linear \gls{eit} reconstruction, establishing figures of merit for evaluating reconstruction quality including amplitude response, position error, resolution, and ringing artefacts.

\textcite{wang2020} demonstrated that not all measurements contribute equally to reconstruction quality, proposing an adaptive optimal measurement algorithm for \gls{ert}-based tactile sensors that selectively acquires the most informative electrode patterns. This reduces acquisition time and energy consumption while maintaining acceptable accuracy. However, the approach introduces algorithmic complexity and may miss important information under noisy or unpredictable conditions, as the selection criterion assumes a known noise model.

For the present work, the key data representation is the \emph{voltage difference vector}. The element-wise difference between measured voltages under contact and a no-contact baseline. This 208-dimensional vector encodes spatial touch information without requiring explicit reconstruction, and serves as the input to all ML classifiers.

\subsection{Software Tools: EIDORS}
\label{sec:lit-eit-eidors}

EIDORS is the dominant open-source framework for \gls{eit} simulation and reconstruction \parencite{adler2006}. It provides standardised implementations of forward models, inverse solvers, mesh generation, and electrode configuration tools. \textcite{adler2006} explicitly caution that while EIDORS enables reproducible research, its flexibility introduces risk of misuse, including: inappropriate regularisation parameters, incorrect boundary conditions, or over-interpretation of low-resolution reconstructions which can produce misleading results. These cautions apply equally to ML models trained on EIDORS-generated data, which inherit the assumptions of the forward model (homogeneous background, idealised electrode-substrate contact, and noise-free boundary conditions).

\textcite{chen2023} made perhaps the most significant contribution relevant to the present work, proposing CNN-enhanced Tikhonov Regularisation (CNN-TR) for \gls{eit} pressure distribution reconstruction. Their hybrid approach combines the physical consistency of classical regularisation with the representational power of neural networks, trained on \gls{fem}-simulated data with additive white Gaussian noise at \gls{snr}~=~10~dB. The CNN architecture employs batch normalisation and ELU activations, trained with MSE loss using Adam ($\alpha$~=~0.0001) over 300 epochs. Critically, the authors demonstrate that pure end-to-end ML approaches are more sensitive to noise and modelling errors than hybrid methods.

However, \textcite{chen2023} employ \emph{only} single-component Gaussian noise and do not examine contact impedance variation, temporal drift, or electrode positioning errors. Furthermore, their evaluation uses a single train/test split without cross-validation.

\textcite{lee2019} characterised an ionic--electronic dual-conductivity hydrogel for \gls{eit} sensing, demonstrating positive piezoresistive response in a 16-electrode configuration with a feedforward neural network for touch localisation. Their work establishes that ionic hydrogels produce measurable conductivity \emph{decreases} under mechanical load. However, no quantitative gauge-factor calibration is provided, and the neural network evaluation uses only 500 samples with a single 80/10/10 split, leaving both the precise force-to-conductivity mapping and model robustness under noise uncharacterised.

\textcite{kim2019} demonstrated stable operation over 10,000 writing cycles but observed gradual performance degradation, confirming that long-term drift is an inherent challenge for wearable \gls{eit} systems. They identified temperature fluctuations as a key noise source, with reconstruction accuracy dependent on idealised assumptions of material homogeneity and stable contact. Their ultrathin elastomer substrate (Young's modulus $\sim$150~kPa) informs the Hertzian contact geometry used in the present work's touch class parameterisation.

\textcite{zhang2022fista} proposed using the FISTA (Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm) within the EIDORS framework for multi-touch \gls{eit} reconstruction, achieving MIoU~=~0.84 with fewer than 30 iterations. They argued that classical optimisation is more practical than deep learning for resource-constrained embedded systems, requiring no large dataset and fewer hyperparameters. This perspective provides important context for the present work's classification-focused approach, which is appropriate precisely because the end-user requires a touch \emph{category} rather than a full spatial map.

\section{Machine Learning for Tactile Classification}
\label{sec:lit-ml}

\subsection{Classical Machine Learning Approaches}
\label{sec:lit-ml-classical}

Prior to the widespread adoption of deep learning, classical methods dominated tactile classification. \glspl{svm} \parencite{cortes1995} have been particularly successful due to their effectiveness in high-dimensional spaces with limited training data. \textcite{osborn2016} achieved near-perfect accuracy with \gls{svm} (RBF kernel) on spike-encoded tactile features, and \textcite{fang2022} report SVMs achieving 96.7\% accuracy for prosthetic alignment correction with proper 5-fold cross-validation.

\glspl{rf} \parencite{breiman2001} offer complementary advantages. They exhibit inherent feature importance ranking, resistance to overfitting through ensemble averaging, and no requirement for feature scaling. These properties make them well-suited for ablation studies where understanding which input features drive classification performance is a primary objective.

\glspl{mlp} provide a bridge between classical and deep learning. \textcite{liu2023} employed a bagged decision tree for handwriting recognition from strain signals (88.4\% accuracy), emphasising interpretability over model complexity which was appropriate given a limited dataset size. \textcite{lee2019} used a simple feedforward network (192$\rightarrow$300$\rightarrow$50$\rightarrow$20$\rightarrow$3 architecture, tanh activation, SGD with momentum) for \gls{eit} touch localisation, trained on only 500 samples with 80/10/10 splits. While achieving reasonable localisation accuracy, the limited architecture exploration and small dataset size leave substantial room for improvement through modern deep learning approaches.

\subsection{Convolutional Neural Networks for Tactile Data}
\label{sec:lit-ml-cnn}

CNNs have become the dominant architecture for tactile classification due to their ability to learn hierarchical spatial features without manual feature engineering \parencite{lecun2015}. Deep learning's capacity for representation learning \parencite{lecun2015} is particularly valuable for \gls{eit} data, where the relationship between voltage patterns and touch locations is highly nonlinear.

\textcite{zheng2020} applied a 2D CNN to 9$\times$9 pressure images from a printed tactile array, using two convolutional layers (16 kernels each of 5$\times$5 and 3$\times$3) followed by a fully-connected output layer for 6-class fruit recognition. The architecture achieves 91.4\% accuracy on 330 test samples from a total dataset of only 1,530. This sample size raises concerns about generalisation, particularly given the absence of data augmentation or cross-validation.

\textcite{yao2022} employed a CNN for 5-class touch classification on \gls{eit} voltage vectors, reporting 98.7\% accuracy with early stopping but without hyperparameter optimisation, k-fold validation, or noise robustness testing. The high accuracy likely reflects the well-separated nature of the classes rather than genuine model robustness.

For 1D voltage difference vectors, 1D CNNs are architecturally natural, treating the measurement vector as a single-channel signal where convolutional kernels learn local patterns in the voltage response corresponding to spatial contact features. Residual connections \parencite{he2016} can mitigate the vanishing gradient problem in deeper architectures which will be investigated further. This architecture has been less explored in the \gls{eit} tactile literature, representing an opportunity addressed by this dissertation.

\subsection{Deep Learning for EIT Reconstruction}
\label{sec:lit-ml-dl-eit}

Beyond classification, deep learning has been applied to the \gls{eit} reconstruction problem itself. \textcite{hamilton2018} proposed Deep D-bar, combining the mathematical D-bar method with neural network post-processing for real-time \gls{eit} imaging. \textcite{wei2019} introduced an induced-current learning approach for nonlinear reconstruction. Both demonstrate that neural networks can compensate for the ill-posedness of the inverse problem, but both are validated primarily on simulation data with idealised noise models. \textcite{malek2022algorithms} survey the broader landscape of deep learning architectures for \gls{eit}, distinguishing between data-driven approaches (which learn direct voltage-to-image mappings) and model-based (physics-informed) methods that incorporate forward model constraints. They argue that data-driven methods, while fast at inference, generalise poorly to real-world data and lack physical consistency guarantees---making physics-informed approaches more suitable for safety-critical applications such as prosthetics. \textcite{rymarczyk2019comparison} compared ANNs, Elastic Net, LARS, and Gauss--Newton methods for industrial electrical tomography, finding that single-output ANNs (96--10--1 architecture) outperformed multi-output networks, and that increasing model complexity does not guarantee better reconstruction quality.

The distinction between using ML for reconstruction (voltage $\rightarrow$ conductivity image) versus classification (voltage $\rightarrow$ touch class label) is important. The present work adopts the classification approach, bypassing the intermediate reconstruction step entirely. This is motivated by the observation that for prosthetic applications, the end-user requires a touch \emph{category} rather than a full spatial conductivity map---and direct classification avoids compounding the errors inherent in ill-posed reconstruction. \textcite{chen2023} demonstrated that hybrid approaches (physics + ML) outperform pure end-to-end methods; the present work extends this principle by investigating whether noise-augmented training can achieve similar robustness benefits for direct classification without requiring explicit physics-based reconstruction.

\subsection{Methodological Concerns Across the Literature}
\label{sec:lit-ml-concerns}

A systematic assessment of ML methodology across reviewed studies reveals several recurring and interrelated concerns:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inadequate validation.} Many studies use single train/test splits without cross-validation \parencite{yao2022, sun2019, zheng2020, chen2023}, making reported accuracies unreliable estimates of true generalisation performance. Where cross-validation is employed \parencite{osborn2016, luo2021, fang2022}, results are more credible but typically remain within controlled conditions.
    
    \item \textbf{Small and homogeneous datasets.} Dataset sizes range from $\sim$500 to $\sim$5,000 samples in most studies, collected under controlled conditions from single phantoms or test rigs. \textcite{lee2019} used only 500 training samples; \textcite{zheng2020} used 1,200. The resulting models are unlikely to generalise across users, materials, or environmental conditions---yet none of these studies discuss this limitation explicitly.
    
    \item \textbf{Inflated accuracy claims.} Reported accuracies of 91--100\% are common \parencite{yao2022, osborn2016, sun2019} but reflect favourable conditions such as: well-separated classes, noise-free data, and in-distribution test sets. \textcite{osborn2016} acknowledged that performance degrades as indentation noise increases, but did not quantify this systematically.
    
    \item \textbf{Missing ablation and robustness analysis.} The contribution of individual design choices is rarely isolated through ablation. \textcite{hendrycks2019} demonstrated that ImageNet-trained networks suffer up to 40\% accuracy degradation under common corruptions, yet equivalent corruption benchmarks do not exist for \gls{eit} tactile sensing. \textcite{goodfellow2015adversarial} showed that neural network vulnerability to perturbations is a fundamental property of high-dimensional linear behaviour, not merely overfitting---implying that robustness requires explicit architectural or training interventions rather than simply collecting more data.
    
    \item \textbf{No calibration or uncertainty analysis.} \textcite{guo2017calibration} demonstrated that modern neural networks are poorly calibrated---their confidence scores do not reflect true prediction probability. For safety-critical prosthetic applications, calibration is arguably as important as accuracy: a system that is confidently wrong under noise is more dangerous than one that correctly signals uncertainty. No reviewed \gls{eit} study reports calibration metrics (Expected Calibration Error, reliability diagrams), representing a significant gap for deployment readiness.
    
    \item \textbf{No standardised benchmark.} Unlike computer vision (ImageNet, CIFAR) or natural language processing (GLUE), the \gls{eit} tactile sensing community lacks standardised datasets or evaluation protocols, making meaningful cross-study comparison impossible \parencite{jiang2023}.
    
    \item \textbf{Limited architecture exploration.} Most \gls{eit} studies apply a single architecture without comparing alternatives. \textcite{caruana2006empirical} demonstrated that model ranking depends heavily on evaluation metric choice, with ensemble methods consistently outperforming single models across diverse tasks---yet no study has systematically compared classical ML baselines (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}) against CNNs under identical noise conditions for \gls{eit} touch classification.
\end{enumerate}

These methodological gaps collectively limit the field's progress toward deployable systems and directly motivate the rigorous, multi-model evaluation framework adopted in this dissertation.

\section{The Simulation-to-Reality Gap}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real}

\subsection{Origin of the Gap in EIT Systems}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-origin}

The simulation-to-reality gap arises when models trained on computationally generated data fail to maintain performance on data from physical hardware. \textcite{ben-david2010domain} formalise this through domain adaptation theory: a classifier's target-domain error is bounded by the source-domain error plus the distributional divergence between domains plus an irreducible joint error. This theoretical framework establishes that reducing domain divergence---whether through noise augmentation, feature alignment, or explicit adaptation---directly improves generalisation guarantees. In \gls{eit}-based tactile sensing, the gap has multiple compounding sources:

\paragraph{Noise characteristics.} Real \gls{eit} systems exhibit noise with complex statistical structure. \textcite{adler2006} report measurement noise at \gls{snr} levels of 40--60~dB for typical instrumentation. \textcite{vilhunen2002} demonstrated that electrode-skin contact impedance introduces multiplicative, spatially-varying distortions distinct from additive Gaussian noise, requiring simultaneous reconstruction of both internal properties and contact impedances. \textcite{boone1996} documented systematic temporal drift in \gls{eit} measurements due to skin impedance changes and showed via model studies that these effects significantly degrade image quality. \textcite{kolehmainen1997} quantified the impact of electrode positioning errors, demonstrating that even small misalignments produce systematic bias in reconstructed conductivity. These sources compound in ways that single-component Gaussian models cannot capture.

\paragraph{Material non-idealities.} Simulation assumes spatially homogeneous, temporally stable conductivity distributions. Real substrates exhibit moisture-dependent conductivity changes \parencite{yao2022}, temperature sensitivity \parencite{kim2019}, and mechanical fatigue \parencite{kim2019}. For ionic hydrogels specifically, the baseline conductivity depends on hydration state and ion concentration, both of which drift over hours of use \parencite{sun2019}. Furthermore, the piezoresistive response itself may be nonlinear at high strains, whereas the present simulation assumes a uniform conductivity within the contact region. \textcite{sun2019} noted that resistivity changes under dehydration or temperature extremes affect both sensing accuracy and the validity of baseline calibration. The progressive nature of these changes means that a model validated immediately after calibration may degrade substantially within hours or days of deployment.

\paragraph{Model simplifications.} Forward models in EIDORS typically use 2D approximations of 3D geometries, idealised electrode models (point or complete electrode model), and homogeneous background conductivity assumptions \parencite{adler2006}. Each simplification introduces systematic bias between simulated and real measurements that cannot be compensated through random noise augmentation alone.

\subsection{Domain Adaptation and Randomisation}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-da}

The computer vision and robotics communities have developed several techniques for bridging simulation-to-reality gaps. \textcite{tobin2017} introduced domain randomisation---randomising rendering parameters (textures, lighting, camera position) during simulation---demonstrating successful transfer of object detection models from simulation to physical robots without any real-world training data. The underlying principle proposes that if sufficient variability is introduced during simulation, the real-world distribution becomes a subset of the training distribution.

\textcite{ganin2016} proposed domain-adversarial training, where a feature extractor is jointly optimised to be discriminative for the primary task while being indistinguishable between source (simulation) and target (reality) domains. \textcite{tzeng2017adda} extended this with Adversarial Discriminative Domain Adaptation (ADDA), using untied source and target encoders with a GAN-style discriminator to align latent feature distributions without requiring target-domain labels. \textcite{pan2010survey} provide a comprehensive taxonomy of transfer learning approaches, distinguishing between instance-based, feature-based, parameter-based, and relational transfer---of which noise augmentation (the present approach) most closely resembles instance-based transfer via importance-weighted training distributions. ADDA's modular architecture makes it particularly applicable to \gls{eit} sensing, where labelled real data are expensive to collect but unlabelled measurements from physical hardware are readily available. However, neither approach has been applied to \gls{eit} tactile sensing, representing an open research direction requiring paired simulation and real data.

\textcite{hendrycks2019} introduced ImageNet-C, a corruption benchmark comprising 15 types (noise, blur, weather, digital) at 5 severity levels. They find that state-of-the-art classifiers suffer catastrophic accuracy drops under common corruptions that humans handle trivially, establishing the importance of systematic robustness evaluation. \textcite{rusak2020simple} subsequently demonstrated that simple Gaussian noise augmentation provides limited corruption robustness; diverse, physically-grounded augmentation strategies substantially outperform single-type augmentation---directly supporting the multi-component approach adopted in this dissertation. This paradigm of multi-type, multi-severity corruption testing directly inspired the evaluation framework of the present study, adapted to \gls{eit}-specific noise sources.

\paragraph{Evidence from adjacent sensing domains.} The principle that simulation-trained models benefit from noise augmentation or domain adaptation has been validated in related fields sharing \gls{eit}'s core challenge of ill-posed inverse problems with measurement noise. Three specific insights transfer directly to the present work. First, \textcite{ford2019adversarial} demonstrated that augmentation \emph{diversity}---rather than magnitude---determines corruption robustness, establishing the principle that multi-component noise should outperform single-component augmentation at any intensity. This directly motivates the four-component design over the single-Gaussian approach of prior \gls{eit} work. Second, \textcite{martin1995error} showed that neural networks trained with noisy \gls{eit} data generalise significantly better to unseen noise conditions but at the cost of degraded clean-domain performance---explicitly quantifying the robustness--precision trade-off that the present work extends to multiple noise types simultaneously. Third, \textcite{pan2010survey} identified that distributional divergence (measured by metrics such as MMD) \emph{predicts} adaptation success across sensor modalities where real data is scarce---a finding that motivates the present work's use of MMD as a diagnostic for per-component gap contribution. These precedents from domains sharing \gls{eit}'s characteristic challenges---expensive real-data labelling, structured noise, and forward-model fidelity---provide direct evidence that noise-matched training should transfer to \gls{eit} tactile sensing, while simultaneously identifying specific design principles (diversity over magnitude, per-component quantification, explicit trade-off measurement) that inform the experimental framework.

\subsection{Noise Augmentation as a Transfer Strategy}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-noise}

Data augmentation during training has long been recognised as an effective regularisation strategy \parencite{shorten2019}. For \gls{eit} specifically, augmenting simulated data with realistic noise represents a form of domain randomisation. If the noise model captures the dominant distortions present in real hardware, models trained on augmented data should generalise better to physical systems.

However, the noise models employed in prior \gls{eit} studies are overwhelmingly simplistic. \textcite{chen2023} add white Gaussian noise at a fixed \gls{snr} during CNN-TR training. \textcite{yao2022} augment with Gaussian noise and background (no-touch) samples. Neither considers contact impedance variation, temporal drift, electrode bias, or quantisation effects. Critically, \textcite{martin1995error} demonstrated that neural networks trained with noisy \gls{eit} data achieve substantially better performance under noisy conditions but at the cost of significant degradation on clean data. Their results indicate an order-of-magnitude trade-off between noise robustness and clean-data precision. This reveals a fundamental robustness-versus-precision trade-off that no subsequent work has attempted to optimise through multi-component noise design. Furthermore, no prior work systematically investigates \emph{which} noise components are most critical for robust training.

This gap is significant because, as \textcite{zheng2020noise} demonstrate in the image domain, noise-augmented training can improve out-of-distribution robustness substantially, but the benefit depends critically on the \emph{correspondence} between training-time augmentation and test-time corruption. Training with unrealistic noise can actually \emph{harm} clean-data performance without corresponding robustness gains, necessitating careful, physically-motivated noise model design. Quantifying this correspondence---the \emph{domain gap}---is itself an open problem; \textcite{gretton2012kernel} developed the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) as a principled two-sample test for measuring distributional divergence in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, providing a formal metric for how far noisy measurements deviate from clean simulation. The present work adopts MMD alongside per-feature Wasserstein distance and correlation structure analysis to quantify the domain gap introduced by each noise component, informing which components contribute most to the simulation-to-reality divergence. \textcite{cubuk2019autoaugment} demonstrated that augmentation strategies can be learned automatically from data while \textcite{devries2017cutout} showed that even simple structured information removal acts as effective regularisation. These findings from computer vision inform the present work's approach of deriving noise parameters from experimental \gls{eit} literature rather than using arbitrary augmentation, while the principle that augmentation type must match deployment conditions motivates physically-grounded noise model design.

\subsection{Limitations and Counter-Arguments for Noise Augmentation}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-counterargs}

It is important to acknowledge conditions under which noise augmentation may fail or provide diminishing returns, as these inform the design choices of the present work.

\paragraph{The precision--robustness trade-off.} \textcite{martin1995error} demonstrated that noise-trained networks degrade substantially on clean data. This finding is consistent with the broader observation that models optimised for worst-case robustness sacrifice average-case accuracy. If the deployment environment is well-controlled (e.g., laboratory settings with frequent recalibration), noise augmentation may reduce overall performance without compensating benefit. The present work addresses this by evaluating performance across a \emph{range} of severity levels, enabling practitioners to select the augmentation intensity appropriate for their deployment conditions rather than assuming maximal noise is always beneficial.

\paragraph{Model mismatch.} Noise augmentation assumes that the training-time noise distribution covers the test-time noise distribution. If the noise model omits a dominant real-world distortion (e.g., electrode corrosion) or misspecifies its statistics, augmented training provides a false sense of robustness. \textcite{zheng2020noise} showed that augmenting with one corruption type provides limited protection against \emph{other} corruption types. The present work mitigates this risk by incorporating five physically-motivated components derived from experimental literature, but acknowledges that unmeasured distortions (e.g., electromagnetic interference, non-stationary contact dynamics) remain outside the model's scope.

\paragraph{Computational cost.} Augmentation increases effective training set size and training time. For resource-constrained embedded prosthetic systems, this offline cost is acceptable since augmentation occurs only during training. However, the approach does not address online adaptation. If the noise distribution shifts after deployment (e.g., material ageing over months), the trained model cannot adapt without retraining.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.} Domain adaptation methods \parencite{ganin2016, tzeng2017adda} offer a complementary strategy that does not require an explicit noise model, instead learning domain-invariant representations directly from unlabelled target data. These approaches may be superior when the true noise characteristics are unknown or too complex to model parametrically. The present work positions noise augmentation as a \emph{first step} that does not require real hardware data, while acknowledging that domain adaptation represents a natural extension when real measurements become available.

\subsection{Thermal and Environmental Effects}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-thermal}

An additional dimension of the simulation-to-reality gap, often overlooked in \gls{eit} studies, is the influence of thermal variation. \textcite{zhang2023thermal} reviewed biomimetic intelligent thermal management materials, noting that biological datasets exhibit high dimensionality, strong nonlinearity, and extreme sensitivity to small parameter changes. They demonstrated that temporal convolutional networks (TCNs) can predict temperature--deformation hysteresis in shape-memory materials, suggesting that temporal deep learning architectures may be valuable for compensating thermal drift in \gls{eit} skins.

For hydrogel-based systems, temperature affects ionic mobility and hence baseline conductivity. \textcite{kim2019} identified temperature fluctuations as a key noise source in wearable \gls{eit} systems. While the present work does not model thermal effects explicitly, these temporal phenomena (drift, thermal variation) represent deployment challenges that require sequential data and temporal modelling approaches which is identified as future work in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}.

\section{Deployment Considerations}
\label{sec:lit-deployment}

\subsection{Scalability and Power Constraints}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-scale}

Prosthetic skin systems must operate within tight power, weight, and computational budgets. \textcite{wang2020} demonstrated that adaptive measurement strategies can reduce energy consumption without sacrificing acceptable accuracy. \textcite{zhang2022fista} argued that classical algorithms (FISTA: $<$30 iterations) are more deployment-appropriate than deep learning for embedded systems. These constraints inform the present work's comparison of lightweight classifiers (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}) against deeper CNN architectures, providing practical guidance on model selection for resource-constrained systems.

The scalability challenge extends beyond computation. \textcite{lee2020} noted that increasing sensor density leads to more wiring and reduced flexibility, while \textcite{jiang2023} highlighted that stretchable interconnects and geometrical designs limit sensor packing density. \gls{eit}'s ability to provide distributed sensing from few boundary electrodes directly addresses this scalability challenge \parencite{silvera-tawil2015}, though at the cost of ill-posed reconstruction.

\subsection{Long-term Reliability and Drift}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-drift}

Long-term operational stability is a prerequisite for prosthetic deployment, yet most studies evaluate only short-term performance. \textcite{kim2019} tested over 10,000 cycles and observed gradual degradation due to material fatigue and conductivity changes, confirming that sensor drift is unavoidable rather than merely a risk. \textcite{lee2019} showed that self-healing materials can restore functionality after damage, but the healing process itself may alter calibration. No study proposes or validates learning-based compensation for progressive drift. This is a significant gap given that recalibration is impractical for daily prosthetic use.

\subsection{Ethical and Societal Considerations}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-ethics}

The development of tactile-sensing prosthetics raises important ethical considerations that the literature largely overlooks. First, the restoration of touch sensation has documented psychological benefits for amputees \parencite{biddiss2007}, but also raises questions about sensory overload, phantom limb interaction, and user consent for neural interfacing \parencite{chortos2016}. Second, the economic accessibility of advanced prosthetic systems remains limited. \textcite{mcdonald2021} document that limb loss disproportionately affects low- and middle-income populations, yet the sensing technologies reviewed here may exacerbate rather than reduce healthcare inequalities. Third, the environmental impact of electronic skin materials (hydrogels, conductive elastomers, carbon nanomaterials) is rarely discussed. Given the disposable nature of prosthetic liners and the eventual degradation of sensor substrates, lifecycle analysis is warranted.

The present work addresses accessibility indirectly by developing an open-source pipeline using freely available tools (EIDORS, PyTorch), lowering the barrier to future research. However, the ethical dimensions of prosthetic sensing technology deserve more systematic attention from the research community than they currently receive.

Three further gaps---long-term drift compensation, inter-user variability, and multi-touch classification---are identified as important but beyond the scope of this dissertation, which focuses on the foundational question of noise robustness for single-contact classification.

\paragraph{A note on publication bias.} The overwhelmingly positive results reported in the reviewed literature (accuracies of 91--100\%) warrant scepticism. Publication bias favours positive outcomes; studies where noise models fail or where classifiers do not outperform baselines are unlikely to appear in the published record. This creates a distorted landscape where the difficulty of the deployment challenge may be substantially underestimated. The present work addresses this bias directly by reporting all experimental conditions---including those where noise augmentation provides no benefit or degrades performance---and by providing effect sizes alongside $p$-values to distinguish statistical from practical significance.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} Taken together, the literature reveals a field that has demonstrated the \emph{feasibility} of ML-enabled EIT tactile sensing but has not yet established its \emph{reliability}. The gap between controlled laboratory demonstrations (98.7\% accuracy) and deployment requirements (robust operation under months of use with multiple noise sources) remains largely unquantified. This dissertation provides the first systematic quantification of that gap through a controlled simulation framework---including formal domain gap metrics \parencite{gretton2012kernel}, confidence calibration analysis \parencite{guo2017calibration}, per-class vulnerability profiling, and noise parameter sensitivity verification---establishing a baseline against which future hardware-validated work can measure progress.
