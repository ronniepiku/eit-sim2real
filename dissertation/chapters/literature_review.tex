\section{Electronic Skin for Prosthetic Applications}
\label{sec:lit-eskin}

\subsection{Biological Motivation and Clinical Need}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-bio}

The clinical demand for tactile-capable prosthetics motivating this work (Section~\ref{sec:intro-background}) has driven extensive research into e-skin platforms. The core engineering challenge is replicating biological skin's combination of distributed multimodal sensing, mechanical compliance, and self-healing within a manufacturable substrate \parencite{chortos2016, hammock2013}.

Building on the clinical motivation outlined in the introduction, restoring tactile sensation requires electronic skins that combine: (i)~mechanical compliance and durability; (ii)~multimodal sensing across pressure, temperature, and texture; (iii)~high spatial resolution over large areas; and (iv)~robustness to long-term drift \parencite{chortos2016, lee2020}.

\textcite{fang2022} reviewed machine learning applications in prosthetics and orthotics, reporting strong performance (SVMs at 96.7\% with 5-fold cross-validation for prosthetic socket alignment). However, inconsistent methodological reporting, particularly regarding patient demographics and dataset characteristics, limits the assessment of cross-user generalisability.

\subsection{Material Platforms for Electronic Skin}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-materials}

\textbf{Hydrogels} offer the closest approximation to biological tissue in compliance and biocompatibility \parencite{park2022skin, sun2019}. Ionic hydrogels exhibit a \emph{positive piezoresistive response}: compression disrupts ion-transport pathways in the hydrated matrix, increasing local resistance under load \parencite{costacornella2023}. This is the inverse of carbon-black-filled elastomers, where compression increases inter-particle contact and decreases resistance. The distinction matters for impedance sensing because the sign and magnitude of the conductivity change determine the resulting boundary voltage patterns. \textcite{park2022skin} report 98.7\% touch classification on a hydrogel--elastomer hybrid using a CNN over \gls{eit} voltage-difference vectors, and \textcite{sun2019} demonstrate material and texture classification at 95--97\%. \textcite{dong2025lattice} show that silicone encapsulation of a lattice-structured hydrogel improves sensitivity and mitigates the fragility of hydrogel-only designs---though testing extends only to approximately 100 cycles, and because the lattice deliberately reshapes current density, part of the gain may reflect hardware amplification rather than algorithmic robustness. The piezoresistive characterisation of \textcite{costacornella2023} and the force ranges of \textcite{zhang2022fista} (50--200\,g) inform the conductivity parametrisation adopted in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}.

\textbf{Conductive elastomers} (carbon-filled silicone) eliminate moisture dependence at the cost of a non-linear response. \textcite{park2022} demonstrate adaptive sensitivity via granular jamming, and \textcite{costacornella2023} achieve a 500\% sensitivity improvement through multi-material anisotropic conductivity with self-healing. \textcite{kim2019} validate stability over 10,000 cycles but document gradual fatigue-driven degradation, confirming that even the most durable platform cannot eliminate drift. \textbf{Printed flexible electronics} \parencite{zheng2020} reach 388.5~kPa$^{-1}$ sensitivity with manufacturing scalability but are limited to low-resolution discrete arrays (81 taxels) incompatible with distributed \gls{eit} sensing.

The central insight is that while each platform introduces \emph{different} dominant material noise---moisture-driven ionic drift, fatigue creep, discrete contact-resistance variation---the measurement instrumentation contributes a \emph{shared} set of per-frame distortions regardless of substrate: \gls{adc} quantisation, electrode--substrate contact impedance, positioning uncertainty and thermal noise. This two-level taxonomy justifies targeting the shared measurement-chain distortions, giving cross-platform generalisability without substrate-specific retraining. The material-dependent temporal effects require sequential data and are deferred to future work.

The choice of modality determines the nature of the sim-to-real challenge. Piezoresistive and capacitive arrays suffer discrete sensor failures and interconnect degradation, whereas \gls{eit} faces a fundamentally different problem: because the inverse problem is ill-posed, noise corrupts not individual sensors but the \emph{entire} spatial reconstruction, propagating errors non-locally. This motivates modality-specific noise models rather than generic augmentation. \textcite{luo2021} demonstrate Ruffini-inspired sensing using fibre Bragg gratings with deep networks, and their methodological rigour---clear task separation, detailed architectures, real-time inference---sets a benchmark many \gls{eit} studies have not matched; but FBG systems require expensive optical interrogators. \gls{eit} needs only current injection and voltage measurement \parencite{silvera-tawil2015}, and its lower spatial resolution is acceptable when the required output is a touch \emph{category} rather than a full spatial map.

\textcite{jiang2023} argue that progress in ML-enabled e-skin depends on overcoming hardware constraints, improving durability, and developing pipelines robust to noise, drift and limited data---challenges \gls{eit} faces acutely given its ill-posedness. \textcite{silvera-tawil2012eit} demonstrated one of the earliest \gls{eit} touch-modality classifiers, using LogitBoost over handcrafted features from 224 boundary measurements at 60~Hz. That work established the feasibility of direct classification without intermediate reconstruction, but relied on handcrafted rather than learned representations.

\subsection{Bio-inspired Signal Encoding and Multimodal Perception}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-bioinspired}

\textcite{osborn2016} proposed a biomimetic circuit mimicking afferent mechanoreceptor firing patterns and reported near-perfect accuracy under controlled laboratory conditions---a result revisited as a methodological exemplar in Section~\ref{sec:lit-ml-concerns}, since even well-chosen representations do not guarantee robustness when evaluated only in-distribution. \textcite{lee2020} additionally highlight the increasing use of recurrent architectures for sequential contact dynamics, a temporal dimension largely absent from \gls{eit} tactile studies and identified here as future work (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}).

\section{Electrical Impedance Tomography for Tactile Sensing}
\label{sec:lit-eit}

\subsection{Principles of EIT}
\label{sec:lit-eit-principles}

\gls{eit} reconstructs the internal conductivity distribution $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ of a body $\Omega$ from boundary voltage measurements. Small alternating currents are injected through pairs of electrodes at the boundary $\partial\Omega$, and the resulting voltage differences are measured across the remaining pairs. The relationship between conductivity and boundary measurements is governed by the generalised Laplace equation:

\begin{equation}
\nabla \cdot (\sigma(\mathbf{x}) \nabla u(\mathbf{x})) = 0, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega
\label{eq:eit-forward}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $u(\mathbf{x})$ is the electric potential. The forward problem is well-posed and solved using \gls{fem} \parencite{cheney1999, adler2006}. The inverse problem---recovering $\sigma$ from boundary measurements---is ill-posed, meaning small measurement errors can produce large, non-physical reconstruction artefacts, with reconstruction quality strongly dependent on electrode configuration, measurement noise, and model assumptions \parencite{cheney1999}. \textcite{liu2022advances} survey the progressive shift in \gls{eit} solvers from traditional regularisation to deep learning, observing that hybrid methods combining physical constraints with learned representations offer the best trade-off between stability and accuracy.

\subsection{EIT Measurement Patterns and Data Representation}
\label{sec:lit-eit-patterns}

For a system with $n_e$ electrodes, the adjacent drive pattern injects current through neighbouring electrode pairs and measures voltages across all remaining pairs, yielding $n_e(n_e-3)$ measurements per frame. A 16-electrode system thus produces $16 \times 13 = 208$ voltage measurements \parencite{adler2009}. The GREIT framework \parencite{adler2009} introduced a unified approach to linear \gls{eit} reconstruction, establishing figures of merit for evaluating reconstruction quality including amplitude response, position error, resolution, and ringing artefacts.

\textcite{wang2020} demonstrated that not all measurements contribute equally to reconstruction quality, proposing an adaptive optimal measurement algorithm for \gls{ert}-based tactile sensors that selectively acquires the most informative electrode patterns. This reduces acquisition time and energy consumption while maintaining acceptable accuracy. However, the approach introduces algorithmic complexity and may miss important information under noisy or unpredictable conditions, as the selection criterion assumes a known noise model.

For the present work, the key data representation is the \emph{voltage difference vector}. The element-wise difference between measured voltages under contact and a no-contact baseline. This 208-dimensional vector encodes spatial touch information without requiring explicit reconstruction, and serves as the input to all ML classifiers.

\subsection{EIDORS}
\label{sec:lit-eit-eidors}

EIDORS is an open-source framework for \gls{eit} simulation and reconstruction \parencite{adler2006}. It provides standardised implementations of forward models, inverse solvers, mesh generation, and electrode configuration tools. \textcite{adler2006} explicitly caution that while EIDORS enables reproducible research, its flexibility introduces risk of misuse, including: inappropriate regularisation parameters, incorrect boundary conditions, or over-interpretation of low-resolution reconstructions which can produce misleading results. These cautions apply equally to ML models trained on EIDORS-generated data, which inherit the assumptions of the forward model (homogeneous background, idealised electrode-substrate contact, and noise-free boundary conditions). The present work's mitigation of these assumptions is detailed in Section~\ref{sec:method-mesh} and discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc-approx-error}.

\textcite{chen2023} is the contribution most directly relevant here, proposing CNN-enhanced Tikhonov Regularisation for \gls{eit} pressure reconstruction. The hybrid design combines the physical consistency of classical regularisation with the representational power of neural networks, and demonstrates that pure end-to-end ML is more noise-sensitive than hybrid methods. However, it is trained with single-component additive Gaussian noise at \gls{snr}~=~10~dB and evaluated on a single train/test split without cross-validation, leaving deployment-relevant robustness unmeasured.

\textcite{dong2025lattice} show that sensor morphology alters \gls{eit} observability before any ML optimisation: a lattice architecture optimised by 3D coupling-field simulation concentrates local current and improves 12-class classification to a reported 99.6\%. This complements algorithmic work but complicates cross-paper comparison, since morphology gains are not commensurable with gains from noise-aware training on a fixed sensor; their augmentation also remains Gaussian-only.

\textcite{costacornella2023} characterise an ionic--electronic dual-conductivity hydrogel in a 16-electrode configuration with a feedforward network for localisation, establishing that ionic hydrogels produce measurable conductivity \emph{decreases} under load. No gauge-factor calibration is provided, and evaluation uses 500 samples on a single 80/10/10 split, leaving both the force-to-conductivity mapping and noise robustness uncharacterised. \textcite{kim2019} demonstrate stable operation over 10,000 cycles but observe gradual degradation, identifying temperature as a key noise source; their ultrathin elastomer ($E \sim$150~kPa) informs the Hertzian contact geometry used in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}. \textcite{zhang2022fista} argue that classical optimisation (FISTA) is more practical than deep learning for embedded systems, requiring no large dataset and fewer hyperparameters.

\section{Machine Learning for Tactile Classification}
\label{sec:lit-ml}

\subsection{Classical Machine Learning Approaches}
\label{sec:lit-ml-classical}

Classical methods dominated tactile classification before deep learning. \glspl{svm} \parencite{cortes1995} are effective in high-dimensional spaces with limited data: \textcite{osborn2016} report near-perfect accuracy on spike-encoded features and \textcite{fang2022} 96.7\% for prosthetic alignment with 5-fold cross-validation. \glspl{rf} \parencite{breiman2001} offer complementary advantages---feature importance ranking, resistance to overfitting through ensemble averaging, and scale invariance.

\glspl{mlp} bridge classical and deep learning. \textcite{costacornella2023} used a feedforward network (192$\rightarrow$300$\rightarrow$50$\rightarrow$20$\rightarrow$3, tanh, SGD with momentum) for \gls{eit} localisation on 500 samples with a single 80/10/10 split. Accuracy on clean data was reasonable, but with no noise, no ablation and no repeated splits, it remains unknown whether such compact architectures survive real measurement distortions.

\subsection{Convolutional Neural Networks for Tactile Data}
\label{sec:lit-ml-cnn}

CNNs dominate tactile classification through their capacity to learn hierarchical features without manual engineering \parencite{lecun2015}, which suits \gls{eit} data where the mapping from voltage patterns to contact is highly nonlinear. \textcite{park2022skin} report 98.7\% on 5-class touch classification from \gls{eit} voltage vectors, but without hyperparameter optimisation, k-fold validation or noise testing; the accuracy likely reflects well-separated classes rather than robustness.

For 1D voltage-difference vectors a 1D-CNN is architecturally natural, treating the measurement as a single-channel signal in which kernels learn local patterns across adjacent electrode pairs. Residual connections \parencite{he2016} are a candidate extension for deeper variants.

\subsection{Deep Learning for EIT Reconstruction}
\label{sec:lit-ml-dl-eit}

Deep learning has also been applied to \gls{eit} reconstruction itself \parencite{hamilton2018, wei2019}, though validated primarily on simulation with idealised noise. \textcite{malek2022algorithms} distinguish data-driven approaches, which learn direct voltage-to-image mappings, from model-based methods incorporating forward-model constraints, arguing that the former generalise poorly and lack physical consistency guarantees---making physics-informed approaches preferable for safety-critical use.

The distinction between reconstruction (voltage~$\rightarrow$~conductivity image) and classification (voltage~$\rightarrow$~class label) matters. This work adopts classification, bypassing reconstruction entirely, because a prosthetic controller requires a touch \emph{category} rather than a spatial map. \textcite{chen2023} showed hybrid physics-plus-ML approaches outperform pure end-to-end methods; the present work asks whether noise-aware training can deliver comparable robustness for direct classification without explicit physics-based reconstruction.

\subsection{Methodological Concerns Across the Literature}
\label{sec:lit-ml-concerns}

A systematic assessment of the ML methodology across the reviewed studies reveals several recurring concerns:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inadequate validation.} Many studies use single train/test splits without cross-validation \parencite{park2022skin, sun2019, zheng2020, chen2023}, making reported accuracies unreliable estimates of generalisation. Where cross-validation is used \parencite{osborn2016, luo2021, fang2022}, results are more credible but remain within controlled conditions.

    \item \textbf{Homogeneous training conditions.} Datasets are typically collected from single phantoms under controlled conditions (\textcite{costacornella2023}: 500 samples; \textcite{zheng2020}: 1,200), with no variability in noise or material state. Models are then evaluated on data drawn from the same distribution, providing no evidence of deployment generalisation.

    \item \textbf{Missing ablation and robustness analysis.} Individual design choices are rarely isolated. \textcite{hendrycks2019} showed ImageNet-trained networks degrade by up to 40\% under common corruptions, yet no equivalent benchmark exists for \gls{eit}. \textcite{goodfellow2015adversarial} established that perturbation vulnerability is a fundamental property of high-dimensional models rather than mere overfitting, implying robustness requires explicit intervention rather than more data.

    \item \textbf{No standardised benchmark.} Unlike computer vision or NLP, the \gls{eit} community lacks standard datasets or protocols, making cross-study comparison unreliable \parencite{jiang2023}.

    \item \textbf{Limited architecture exploration.} Most studies apply one architecture without comparison. \textcite{caruana2006empirical} showed model ranking depends heavily on evaluation choice, yet no study has compared classical baselines against CNNs under identical noise conditions for \gls{eit} touch classification.
\end{enumerate}

These gaps motivate the multi-model, multi-condition evaluation framework adopted here. The third is the most consequential for this dissertation: as Chapter~\ref{ch:results} shows, how an ablation is evaluated determines which components it identifies as important.

\section{The Simulation-to-Reality Gap}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real}

\subsection{Origin of the Gap in EIT Systems}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-origin}

The simulation-to-reality gap arises when models trained on computationally generated data fail to maintain performance on data from physical hardware. \textcite{ben-david2010domain} formalise this through domain adaptation theory, where a classifier's target-domain error is bounded by the source-domain error plus the distributional divergence between domains plus an irreducible joint error. This theoretical framework establishes that reducing domain divergence directly improves generalisation guarantees. In \gls{eit}-based tactile sensing, the gap has multiple compounding sources:

\paragraph{Noise characteristics.} Real \gls{eit} systems exhibit noise with complex statistical structure. \textcite{adler2006} report measurement noise at \gls{snr} levels of 40--60~dB for typical instrumentation. \textcite{vilhunen2002} demonstrated that electrode-skin contact impedance introduces multiplicative, spatially-varying distortions distinct from additive Gaussian noise, requiring simultaneous reconstruction of both internal properties and contact impedances. \textcite{boone1996} documented systematic temporal drift in \gls{eit} measurements due to skin impedance changes and showed via model studies that these effects significantly degrade image quality. \textcite{kolehmainen1997} quantified the impact of electrode positioning errors, demonstrating that even small misalignments produce systematic bias in reconstructed conductivity. These sources compound in ways that single-component Gaussian models cannot capture.

\paragraph{Material non-idealities.} Simulation assumes homogeneous, temporally stable conductivity. Real substrates exhibit moisture-dependent changes \parencite{park2022skin, sun2019}, temperature sensitivity and mechanical fatigue \parencite{kim2019}. For ionic hydrogels, baseline conductivity depends on hydration and ion concentration, both drifting over hours, so a model validated immediately after calibration may degrade within days.

\paragraph{Model simplifications.} EIDORS forward models typically use 2D approximations of 3D geometries, idealised electrode models and homogeneous background assumptions \parencite{adler2006}. Each introduces systematic bias that random noise augmentation cannot compensate---a point developed in Section~\ref{sec:disc-approx-error}.

\subsection{Domain Adaptation and Randomisation}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-da}

\textcite{tobin2017} introduced domain randomisation, transferring object detection from simulation to physical robots without real training data. The principle is that if sufficient variability is introduced in simulation, the real distribution becomes a subset of the training distribution---the framing adopted for noise augmentation here.

\textcite{ganin2016} proposed domain-adversarial training, jointly optimising a feature extractor to be task-discriminative while indistinguishable between source and target domains; \textcite{tzeng2017adda} extended this with ADDA, aligning latent distributions without target labels. In the taxonomy of \textcite{pan2010survey}, noise augmentation most closely resembles instance-based transfer. ADDA is particularly applicable to \gls{eit}, where labelled real data are expensive but unlabelled hardware measurements are readily obtained; neither approach, however, has been applied to \gls{eit} tactile sensing, and both require paired data this study does not have.

\textcite{hendrycks2019} introduced ImageNet-C, showing that state-of-the-art classifiers suffer catastrophic drops under corruptions humans handle trivially, and establishing the multi-severity evaluation paradigm adopted in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}. \textcite{rusak2020simple} further showed that simple Gaussian augmentation provides limited corruption robustness, while diverse physically-grounded strategies substantially outperform it---directly motivating the multi-component approach taken here.

\subsection{Noise Augmentation as a Transfer Strategy}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-noise}

Given noise augmentation as a form of domain randomisation, the question is whether existing \gls{eit} noise models are adequate for it. \textcite{chen2023} add white Gaussian noise at fixed \gls{snr}; \textcite{park2022skin} augment with Gaussian noise and background samples. Neither considers contact impedance, electrode bias or quantisation. \textcite{martin1995error} showed that networks trained on noisy \gls{eit} data perform substantially better under noise but degrade on clean data---a robustness-versus-precision trade-off no subsequent work has attempted to optimise. No prior work investigates \emph{which} components are most critical.

This matters because the benefit depends on the correspondence between training-time augmentation and test-time corruption. \textcite{zheng2020noise} showed that augmenting with one corruption type provides limited robustness to others, and that mismatched augmentation can \emph{harm} clean performance without compensating gain---necessitating physically-motivated design rather than arbitrary augmentation.

\subsection{Limitations and Counter-Arguments}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-counterargs}

Three conditions under which noise augmentation may fail warrant attention. First, the \textbf{precision--robustness trade-off} \parencite{martin1995error}: where deployment is well-controlled and frequently recalibrated, augmentation may cost accuracy without compensating benefit. The present work therefore evaluates across a \emph{range} of severities rather than assuming maximal noise is always beneficial. Second, \textbf{model mismatch}: augmentation assumes the training distribution covers the test distribution, so an omitted or misspecified dominant distortion yields only false robustness \parencite{zheng2020noise}. Four physically-motivated components mitigate this, though unmeasured distortions such as electromagnetic interference remain out of scope. Third, \textbf{alternative approaches}: domain adaptation \parencite{ganin2016, tzeng2017adda} learns domain-invariant representations directly from unlabelled target data and may be superior where noise is too complex to model parametrically. Noise augmentation is positioned here as a first step requiring no hardware data, with adaptation the natural extension once real measurements exist.

\section{Deployment Considerations}
\label{sec:lit-deployment}

\subsection{Scalability and Power Constraints}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-scale}

Prosthetic skin systems must operate within tight power, weight, and computational budgets. \textcite{wang2020} demonstrated that adaptive measurement strategies can reduce energy consumption without sacrificing acceptable accuracy. \textcite{zhang2022fista} argued that classical algorithms (FISTA: $<$30 iterations) are more deployment-appropriate than deep learning for embedded systems. These constraints inform the present work's comparison of lightweight classifiers (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}) against deeper CNN architectures, providing practical guidance on model selection for resource-constrained systems.

The scalability challenge extends beyond computation. \textcite{lee2020} noted that increasing sensor density leads to more wiring and reduced flexibility, while \textcite{jiang2023} highlighted that stretchable interconnects and geometrical designs limit sensor packing density. \gls{eit}'s ability to provide distributed sensing from few boundary electrodes directly addresses this scalability challenge \parencite{silvera-tawil2015}, though at the cost of ill-posed reconstruction.

\subsection{Long-term Reliability and Drift}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-drift}

Long-term operational stability is a prerequisite for prosthetic deployment, yet most studies evaluate only short-term performance. \textcite{kim2019} tested over 10,000 cycles and observed gradual degradation due to material fatigue and conductivity changes, confirming that sensor drift is unavoidable rather than merely a risk. \textcite{costacornella2023} showed that self-healing materials can restore functionality after damage, but the healing process itself may alter calibration. No study proposes or validates learning-based compensation for progressive drift. This is a significant gap given that recalibration is impractical for daily prosthetic use.

\subsection{Ethical and Societal Considerations}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-ethics}

The development of tactile-sensing prosthetics raises ethical considerations that the literature largely overlooks. The restoration of touch has documented psychological benefits for amputees \parencite{biddiss2007} but also raises questions about sensory overload and user consent for neural interfacing \parencite{chortos2016}. More pressingly, advanced prosthetic systems remain economically inaccessible to many of those who need them. \textcite{mcdonald2021} document that limb loss disproportionately affects low- and middle-income populations, meaning that high-cost sensing technologies risk widening healthcare inequalities rather than reducing them. The present work addresses accessibility indirectly by developing an open-source pipeline that lowers the barrier to subsequent research and reproduction, though the broader ethical dimensions of prosthetic sensing technology deserve more systematic attention than they currently receive in the engineering literature.

Taken together, the literature reveals a field that has demonstrated the \emph{feasibility} of ML-enabled EIT tactile sensing but has not yet established its \emph{reliability}. The gap between controlled laboratory demonstrations (98.7\% accuracy) and deployment requirements remains largely unquantified. This dissertation quantifies that gap through a controlled simulation framework---decomposing it by noise source, profiling per-class vulnerability, analysing confidence calibration \parencite{guo2017calibration}, and verifying sensitivity to the modelling assumptions---establishing a baseline against which future hardware-validated work can measure progress. Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} details how these gaps are addressed.

\paragraph{A note on publication bias.} The overwhelmingly positive results reported in the reviewed literature (accuracies of 91--100\%) warrant scepticism. Publication bias favours positive outcomes. Studies where noise models fail or where classifiers do not outperform baselines are unlikely to appear in the published record. This creates a distorted landscape where the difficulty of the deployment challenge may be substantially underestimated. The present work addresses this bias directly by reporting all experimental conditions and by providing effect sizes.
