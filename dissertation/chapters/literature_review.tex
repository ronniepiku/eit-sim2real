\section{Electronic Skin for Prosthetic Applications}
\label{sec:lit-eskin}

\subsection{Biological Motivation and Clinical Need}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-bio}

The clinical demand for tactile-capable prosthetics motivating this work (Section~\ref{sec:intro-background}) has driven extensive research into electronic skin platforms. The core engineering challenge is replicating biological skin's combination of distributed multimodal sensing, mechanical compliance, and self-healing within a manufacturable substrate \parencite{chortos2016, hammock2013}.

Building on the clinical motivation outlined in the introduction, restoring tactile sensation requires electronic skins that combine: (i)~mechanical compliance and durability; (ii)~multimodal sensing across pressure, temperature, and texture; (iii)~high spatial resolution over large areas; and (iv)~robustness to long-term drift \parencite{chortos2016, lee2020}.

\textcite{fang2022} reviewed machine learning applications in prosthetics and orthotics, finding strong reported performance (SVMs at 96.7\% with 5-fold cross-validation for prosthetic socket alignment) but inconsistent methodological reporting, particularly regarding patient demographics and dataset characteristics. This limits assessment of cross-user generalisability---a critical requirement for real-world prosthetic deployment.

\subsection{Material Platforms for Electronic Skin}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-materials}

The choice of substrate material fundamentally constrains sensing performance, durability, and wearability. Three dominant material platforms have emerged, each presenting distinct trade-offs that can be evaluated against four criteria critical for prosthetic deployment: \emph{temporal stability} (resistance to drift), \emph{mechanical durability}, \emph{sensing fidelity}, and \emph{manufacturing scalability}. Table~\ref{tab:material-comparison} summarises this comparison.

\textbf{Hydrogels} offer the closest approximation to biological tissue in terms of compliance and biocompatibility \parencite{yao2022, sun2019}. Crucially, ionic hydrogels exhibit a \emph{positive piezoresistive response} where mechanical compression disrupts ion-transport pathways within the hydrated polymer matrix, increasing local resistance (decreasing conductivity) under applied force \parencite{lee2019}. This is the inverse of the response exhibited by carbon-black--filled elastomers, where compression increases inter-particle contact and \emph{decreases} resistance. The ionic mechanism is directly relevant to impedance-based sensing, as the magnitude and sign of the conductivity change determine the resulting boundary voltage patterns (Section~\ref{sec:lit-eit}). \textcite{yao2022} achieved 98.7\% touch classification accuracy on a hydrogel--elastomer hybrid using a CNN operating on \gls{eit} voltage difference vectors, while \textcite{sun2019} demonstrated multifunctional sensing (material and texture classification at 95--97\% accuracy). However, both platforms suffer from moisture-driven baseline drift that neither study addresses, motivating noise-robust training even before temporal effects are considered. The piezoresistive characterisation reported by \textcite{lee2019} and the force ranges documented by \textcite{zhang2022fista} (50--200\,g on hydrogel substrates) inform the conductivity parametrisation adopted in the present work (Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}).

\textbf{Conductive elastomers} (carbon-filled silicone) eliminate moisture dependence at the cost of nonlinear piezoresistive response. \textcite{park2022} demonstrated adaptive sensitivity via granular jamming, and \textcite{lee2019} achieved 500\% sensitivity improvement through multimaterial anisotropic conductivity with self-healing capability. Critically, \textcite{kim2019} validated stability over 10,000 cycles but documented gradual degradation from material fatigue and temperature sensitivity, confirming that even the most durable platform cannot eliminate drift entirely.

\textbf{Printed flexible electronics} \parencite{zheng2020} achieve 388.5~kPa$^{-1}$ sensitivity with manufacturing scalability, but are limited to low-resolution discrete arrays (81 taxels) incompatible with the distributed sensing paradigm of \gls{eit}.

The central insight from this comparison is that each platform introduces \emph{different} dominant noise mechanisms---moisture-driven ionic drift in hydrogels, nonlinear fatigue creep and temperature sensitivity in elastomers, and discrete contact-resistance variation in printed arrays---yet the measurement instrumentation introduces a shared set of per-frame distortions (ADC quantisation, electrode--substrate contact impedance, positioning uncertainty, and electronics thermal noise) regardless of the underlying substrate. This two-level noise taxonomy---material-specific temporal effects versus material-agnostic instrumentation noise---justifies a noise model targeting the shared measurement-chain distortions, enabling cross-platform generalisability without requiring substrate-specific retraining. The material-dependent temporal effects (drift, fatigue, dehydration) require sequential data and are deferred to future work (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}).

Among these, \gls{eit} and fibre-optic approaches stand out for large-area prosthetic applications. The choice of modality also determines the nature of the sim-to-real challenge: piezoresistive and capacitive arrays suffer from discrete sensor failures and interconnect degradation, whereas \gls{eit} faces a fundamentally different problem---the inverse problem's ill-posedness means that noise corrupts not individual sensors but the \emph{entire} spatial reconstruction, propagating measurement errors non-locally across the sensing area. This distinction motivates modality-specific noise models rather than generic data augmentation. \textcite{luo2021} demonstrated Ruffini-receptor-inspired tactile sensing using fibre Bragg gratings (FBGs) with deep neural networks, employing four separate MLPs for contact localisation and a CNN for force estimation; their methodological rigour---clear task separation, detailed architectures, and real-time inference---sets a benchmark that many \gls{eit}-based studies have not yet matched. However, FBG systems require expensive optical interrogators, limiting accessibility. \gls{eit}, by contrast, requires only simple current injection and voltage measurement hardware \parencite{silvera-tawil2015}, and its lower spatial resolution is acceptable when the target output is a touch \emph{category} rather than a full spatial map (Section~\ref{sec:lit-eit-eskin}).

\textcite{jiang2023} argued that ML is essential for unlocking the full potential of advanced e-skin systems, but that progress depends on overcoming hardware constraints, improving material durability, and developing ML pipelines robust to noise, drift, and limited data. This review identifies the same challenges and notes that \gls{eit} skins face these issues acutely due to the ill-posed nature of the inverse problem. Notably, \textcite{silvera-tawil2012eit} demonstrated one of the earliest ML-based touch modality classification systems using \gls{eit}, employing a LogitBoost classifier with handcrafted features (intensity change, contact area, temporal dynamics) extracted from 224 boundary voltage measurements acquired at 60~Hz. This early work established the feasibility of direct classification from \gls{eit} measurements without intermediate reconstruction, but used classical features rather than learned representations, leaving substantial scope for deep learning approaches.

\subsection{Bio-inspired Signal Encoding and Multimodal Perception}
\label{sec:lit-eskin-bioinspired}

\textcite{osborn2016} proposed a biomimetic circuit mimicking afferent mechanoreceptor firing patterns and reported near-perfect accuracy under controlled laboratory conditions---a result revisited as a methodological exemplar in Section~\ref{sec:lit-ml-concerns}, since even well-chosen representations do not guarantee robustness when evaluated only in-distribution. \textcite{lee2020} additionally highlight the increasing use of recurrent architectures for sequential contact dynamics, a temporal dimension largely absent from \gls{eit} tactile studies and identified here as future work (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}).

\section{Electrical Impedance Tomography for Tactile Sensing}
\label{sec:lit-eit}

\subsection{Principles of EIT}
\label{sec:lit-eit-principles}

\gls{eit} reconstructs the internal conductivity distribution $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ of a body $\Omega$ from boundary voltage measurements. Small alternating currents are injected through electrode pairs on the boundary $\partial\Omega$, and the resulting voltage differences are measured across remaining pairs. The relationship between conductivity and boundary measurements is governed by the generalised Laplace equation:

\begin{equation}
\nabla \cdot (\sigma(\mathbf{x}) \nabla u(\mathbf{x})) = 0, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega
\label{eq:eit-forward}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $u(\mathbf{x})$ is the electric potential. The forward problem is well-posed and typically solved using the \gls{fem} \parencite{cheney1999, adler2006}. The inverse problem---recovering $\sigma$ from boundary measurements---is severely ill-posed: small measurement errors can produce large, non-physical reconstruction artefacts, with reconstruction quality strongly dependent on electrode configuration, measurement noise, and model assumptions \parencite{cheney1999}. \textcite{liu2022advances} survey the progressive shift in \gls{eit} solvers from traditional regularisation to deep learning, observing that hybrid methods combining physical constraints with learned representations offer the best trade-off between stability and accuracy---a principle that directly motivates the present work's choice of physically-grounded noise augmentation over purely data-driven robustness training.

\subsection{EIT Measurement Patterns and Data Representation}
\label{sec:lit-eit-patterns}

For a system with $n_e$ electrodes, the adjacent drive pattern injects current through neighbouring electrode pairs and measures voltages across all remaining pairs, yielding $n_e(n_e-3)/2$ independent measurements per frame. A 16-electrode system thus produces 208 independent voltage measurements \parencite{adler2009}. The GREIT framework \parencite{adler2009} introduced a unified approach to linear \gls{eit} reconstruction, establishing figures of merit for evaluating reconstruction quality including amplitude response, position error, resolution, and ringing artefacts.

\textcite{wang2020} demonstrated that not all measurements contribute equally to reconstruction quality, proposing an adaptive optimal measurement algorithm for \gls{ert}-based tactile sensors that selectively acquires the most informative electrode patterns. This reduces acquisition time and energy consumption while maintaining acceptable accuracy. However, the approach introduces algorithmic complexity and may miss important information under noisy or unpredictable conditions, as the selection criterion assumes a known noise model.

For the present work, the key data representation is the \emph{voltage difference vector}. The element-wise difference between measured voltages under contact and a no-contact baseline. This 208-dimensional vector encodes spatial touch information without requiring explicit reconstruction, and serves as the input to all ML classifiers.

\subsection{Software Tools: EIDORS}
\label{sec:lit-eit-eidors}

EIDORS is the dominant open-source framework for \gls{eit} simulation and reconstruction \parencite{adler2006}. It provides standardised implementations of forward models, inverse solvers, mesh generation, and electrode configuration tools. \textcite{adler2006} explicitly caution that while EIDORS enables reproducible research, its flexibility introduces risk of misuse, including: inappropriate regularisation parameters, incorrect boundary conditions, or over-interpretation of low-resolution reconstructions which can produce misleading results. These cautions apply equally to ML models trained on EIDORS-generated data, which inherit the assumptions of the forward model (homogeneous background, idealised electrode-substrate contact, and noise-free boundary conditions). The present work's mitigation of these assumptions is detailed in Section~\ref{sec:method-mesh} and discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc-approx-error}.

\textcite{chen2023} made the contribution most directly relevant to the present work, proposing CNN-enhanced Tikhonov Regularisation (CNN-TR) for \gls{eit} pressure reconstruction. The hybrid design combines classical regularisation's physical consistency with the representational power of neural networks, trained on \gls{fem}-simulated data with additive white Gaussian noise at \gls{snr}~=~10~dB, and demonstrates that pure end-to-end ML is more sensitive to noise than hybrid methods. However, the noise model is restricted to single-component Gaussian---ignoring contact impedance variation, temporal drift, and electrode positioning errors---and evaluation uses a single train/test split without cross-validation, leaving deployment-relevant robustness unmeasured.

\textcite{lee2019} characterised an ionic--electronic dual-conductivity hydrogel for \gls{eit} sensing, demonstrating positive piezoresistive response in a 16-electrode configuration with a feedforward neural network for touch localisation. Their work establishes that ionic hydrogels produce measurable conductivity \emph{decreases} under mechanical load. However, no quantitative gauge-factor calibration is provided, and the neural network evaluation uses only 500 samples with a single 80/10/10 split, leaving both the precise force-to-conductivity mapping and model robustness under noise uncharacterised.

\textcite{kim2019} demonstrated stable operation over 10,000 writing cycles but observed gradual performance degradation, confirming that long-term drift is an inherent challenge for wearable \gls{eit} systems. They identified temperature fluctuations as a key noise source, with reconstruction accuracy dependent on idealised assumptions of material homogeneity and stable contact. Their ultrathin elastomer substrate (Young's modulus $\sim$150~kPa) informs the Hertzian contact geometry used in the present work's touch class parameterisation.

\textcite{zhang2022fista} proposed using the FISTA (Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm) within the EIDORS framework for multi-touch \gls{eit} reconstruction, achieving MIoU~=~0.84 with fewer than 30 iterations. They argued that classical optimisation is more practical than deep learning for resource-constrained embedded systems, requiring no large dataset and fewer hyperparameters. This perspective provides important context for the present work's classification-focused approach, which is appropriate precisely because the end-user requires a touch \emph{category} rather than a full spatial map.

\section{Machine Learning for Tactile Classification}
\label{sec:lit-ml}

\subsection{Classical Machine Learning Approaches}
\label{sec:lit-ml-classical}

Prior to the widespread adoption of deep learning, classical methods dominated tactile classification. \glspl{svm} \parencite{cortes1995} have been particularly successful due to their effectiveness in high-dimensional spaces with limited training data. \textcite{osborn2016} achieved near-perfect accuracy with \gls{svm} (RBF kernel) on spike-encoded tactile features, and \textcite{fang2022} report SVMs achieving 96.7\% accuracy for prosthetic alignment correction with proper 5-fold cross-validation.

\glspl{rf} \parencite{breiman2001} offer complementary advantages. They exhibit inherent feature importance ranking, resistance to overfitting through ensemble averaging, and no requirement for feature scaling. These properties make them well-suited for ablation studies where understanding which input features drive classification performance is a primary objective.

\glspl{mlp} provide a bridge between classical and deep learning. \textcite{lee2019} used a simple feedforward network (192$\rightarrow$300$\rightarrow$50$\rightarrow$20$\rightarrow$3 architecture, tanh activation, SGD with momentum) for \gls{eit} touch localisation, trained on only 500 samples with 80/10/10 splits. While achieving reasonable localisation accuracy on clean data, the evaluation provides no indication of robustness: no noise was introduced, no ablation was performed, and the single split offers no statistical reliability---leaving it unknown whether such compact architectures can maintain performance under the measurement distortions present in real hardware.

\subsection{Convolutional Neural Networks for Tactile Data}
\label{sec:lit-ml-cnn}

CNNs have become the dominant architecture for tactile classification due to their ability to learn hierarchical spatial features without manual feature engineering \parencite{lecun2015}. Deep learning's capacity for representation learning \parencite{lecun2015} is particularly valuable for \gls{eit} data, where the relationship between voltage patterns and touch locations is highly nonlinear.

\textcite{yao2022} employed a CNN for 5-class touch classification on \gls{eit} voltage vectors, reporting 98.7\% accuracy with early stopping but without hyperparameter optimisation, k-fold validation, or noise robustness testing. The high accuracy likely reflects the well-separated nature of the classes rather than genuine model robustness.

For 1D voltage difference vectors, 1D-CNNs are architecturally natural, treating the measurement vector as a single-channel signal where convolutional kernels learn local patterns in the voltage response corresponding to spatial contact features. Residual connections \parencite{he2016} can mitigate the vanishing gradient problem in deeper architectures and are identified as a candidate extension for future work.

\subsection{Deep Learning for EIT Reconstruction}
\label{sec:lit-ml-dl-eit}

Beyond classification, deep learning has been applied to the \gls{eit} reconstruction problem itself \parencite{hamilton2018, wei2019}, but these methods are validated primarily on simulation data with idealised noise models. \textcite{malek2022algorithms} survey the broader landscape, distinguishing between data-driven approaches (which learn direct voltage-to-image mappings) and model-based (physics-informed) methods that incorporate forward model constraints. They argue that data-driven methods, while fast at inference, generalise poorly to real-world data and lack physical consistency guarantees---making physics-informed approaches more suitable for safety-critical applications such as prosthetics.

The distinction between using ML for reconstruction (voltage $\rightarrow$ conductivity image) versus classification (voltage $\rightarrow$ touch class label) is important. The present work adopts the classification approach, bypassing the intermediate reconstruction step entirely. This is motivated by the observation that for prosthetic applications, the end-user requires a touch \emph{category} rather than a full spatial conductivity map---and direct classification avoids compounding the errors inherent in ill-posed reconstruction. \textcite{chen2023} demonstrated that hybrid approaches (physics + ML) outperform pure end-to-end methods; the present work extends this principle by investigating whether noise-augmented training can achieve similar robustness benefits for direct classification without requiring explicit physics-based reconstruction.

\subsection{Methodological Concerns Across the Literature}
\label{sec:lit-ml-concerns}

A systematic assessment of ML methodology across reviewed studies reveals several recurring and interrelated concerns:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inadequate validation.} Many studies use single train/test splits without cross-validation \parencite{yao2022, sun2019, zheng2020, chen2023}, making reported accuracies unreliable estimates of true generalisation performance. Where cross-validation is employed \parencite{osborn2016, luo2021, fang2022}, results are more credible but typically remain within controlled conditions.
    
    \item \textbf{Homogeneous training conditions.} Datasets are typically collected from single phantoms under controlled conditions (\textcite{lee2019}: 500 samples; \textcite{zheng2020}: 1,200), with no variability in noise, material state, or environmental factors. The resulting models are evaluated on data drawn from the same distribution as training, providing no evidence of generalisation to the diverse conditions encountered in deployment.
    
    \item \textbf{Inflated accuracy claims.} Reported accuracies of 91--100\% are common \parencite{yao2022, osborn2016, sun2019} but reflect favourable conditions such as: well-separated classes, noise-free data, and in-distribution test sets. \textcite{osborn2016} acknowledged that performance degrades as indentation noise increases, but did not quantify this systematically.
    
    \item \textbf{Missing ablation and robustness analysis.} The contribution of individual design choices is rarely isolated through ablation. \textcite{hendrycks2019} demonstrated that ImageNet-trained networks suffer up to 40\% accuracy degradation under common corruptions, yet equivalent corruption benchmarks do not exist for \gls{eit} tactile sensing. \textcite{goodfellow2015adversarial} showed that neural network vulnerability to perturbations is a fundamental property of high-dimensional linear behaviour, not merely overfitting---implying that robustness requires explicit architectural or training interventions rather than simply collecting more data.
    
    \item \textbf{No calibration or uncertainty analysis.} \textcite{guo2017calibration} demonstrated that modern neural networks are poorly calibrated---their confidence scores do not reflect true prediction probability. For safety-critical prosthetic applications, calibration is arguably as important as accuracy: a system that is confidently wrong under noise is more dangerous than one that correctly signals uncertainty. No reviewed \gls{eit} study reports calibration metrics (Expected Calibration Error, reliability diagrams), representing a significant gap for deployment readiness.
    
    \item \textbf{No standardised benchmark.} Unlike computer vision (ImageNet, CIFAR) or natural language processing (GLUE), the \gls{eit} tactile sensing community lacks standardised datasets or evaluation protocols, making meaningful cross-study comparison impossible \parencite{jiang2023}.
    
    \item \textbf{Limited architecture exploration.} Most \gls{eit} studies apply a single architecture without comparing alternatives. \textcite{caruana2006empirical} demonstrated that model ranking depends heavily on evaluation metric choice, with ensemble methods consistently outperforming single models across diverse tasks---yet no study has systematically compared classical ML baselines (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}) against CNNs under identical noise conditions for \gls{eit} touch classification.
\end{enumerate}

These methodological gaps collectively limit the field's progress toward deployable systems and directly motivate the rigorous, multi-model evaluation framework adopted in this dissertation.

\section{The Simulation-to-Reality Gap}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real}

\subsection{Origin of the Gap in EIT Systems}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-origin}

The simulation-to-reality gap arises when models trained on computationally generated data fail to maintain performance on data from physical hardware. \textcite{ben-david2010domain} formalise this through domain adaptation theory, where a classifier's target-domain error is bounded by the source-domain error plus the distributional divergence between domains plus an irreducible joint error. This theoretical framework establishes that reducing domain divergence directly improves generalisation guarantees. In \gls{eit}-based tactile sensing, the gap has multiple compounding sources:

\paragraph{Noise characteristics.} Real \gls{eit} systems exhibit noise with complex statistical structure. \textcite{adler2006} report measurement noise at \gls{snr} levels of 40--60~dB for typical instrumentation. \textcite{vilhunen2002} demonstrated that electrode-skin contact impedance introduces multiplicative, spatially-varying distortions distinct from additive Gaussian noise, requiring simultaneous reconstruction of both internal properties and contact impedances. \textcite{boone1996} documented systematic temporal drift in \gls{eit} measurements due to skin impedance changes and showed via model studies that these effects significantly degrade image quality. \textcite{kolehmainen1997} quantified the impact of electrode positioning errors, demonstrating that even small misalignments produce systematic bias in reconstructed conductivity. These sources compound in ways that single-component Gaussian models cannot capture.

\paragraph{Material non-idealities.} Simulation assumes spatially homogeneous, temporally stable conductivity distributions. Real substrates exhibit moisture-dependent conductivity changes \parencite{yao2022, sun2019}, temperature sensitivity, and mechanical fatigue \parencite{kim2019}. For ionic hydrogels specifically, baseline conductivity depends on hydration state and ion concentration, both of which drift over hours of use---meaning a model validated immediately after calibration may degrade substantially within hours or days of deployment. The piezoresistive response may also be nonlinear at high strains, whereas the present simulation assumes uniform contact-region conductivity.

\paragraph{Model simplifications.} Forward models in EIDORS typically use 2D approximations of 3D geometries, idealised electrode models (point or complete electrode model), and homogeneous background conductivity assumptions \parencite{adler2006}. Each simplification introduces systematic bias between simulated and real measurements that cannot be compensated through random noise augmentation alone.

\subsection{Domain Adaptation and Randomisation}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-da}

The computer vision and robotics communities have developed several techniques for bridging simulation-to-reality gaps. \textcite{tobin2017} introduced domain randomisation demonstrating successful transfer of object detection models from simulation to physical robots without any real-world training data. The underlying principle proposes that if sufficient variability is introduced during simulation, the real-world distribution becomes a subset of the training distribution.

\textcite{ganin2016} proposed domain-adversarial training, where a feature extractor is jointly optimised to be discriminative for the primary task while being indistinguishable between source (simulation) and target (reality) domains. \textcite{tzeng2017adda} extended this with Adversarial Discriminative Domain Adaptation (ADDA), using untied source and target encoders with a GAN-style discriminator to align latent feature distributions without requiring target-domain labels. \textcite{pan2010survey} provide a comprehensive taxonomy of transfer learning approaches, distinguishing between instance-based, feature-based, parameter-based, and relational transfer---of which noise augmentation (the present approach) most closely resembles instance-based transfer via importance-weighted training distributions. ADDA's modular architecture makes it particularly applicable to \gls{eit} sensing, where labelled real data are expensive to collect but unlabelled measurements from physical hardware are readily available. However, neither approach has been applied to \gls{eit} tactile sensing, representing an open research direction requiring paired simulation and real data.

\textcite{hendrycks2019} introduced ImageNet-C, a corruption benchmark comprising 15 types (noise, blur, weather, digital) at 5 severity levels. They find that state-of-the-art classifiers suffer catastrophic accuracy drops under common corruptions that humans handle trivially, establishing the importance of systematic robustness evaluation. \textcite{rusak2020simple} subsequently demonstrated that simple Gaussian noise augmentation provides limited corruption robustness; diverse, physically-grounded augmentation strategies substantially outperform single-type augmentation---directly supporting the multi-component approach adopted in this dissertation. This paradigm of multi-type, multi-severity corruption testing directly inspired the evaluation framework of the present study, adapted to \gls{eit}-specific noise sources.

\paragraph{Evidence from adjacent sensing domains.} \textcite{ford2019adversarial} demonstrated that augmentation \emph{diversity}, not magnitude, determines corruption robustness---directly motivating the four-component design adopted here over the single-Gaussian approach of prior \gls{eit} work. \textcite{pan2010survey} additionally established that distributional divergence (measured by metrics such as MMD) predicts adaptation success in modalities where real data are scarce. These precedents from domains sharing \gls{eit}'s characteristic challenges---expensive real-data labelling, structured noise, and forward-model fidelity---justify both the multi-component augmentation design and the use of per-component divergence metrics adopted in this dissertation.

\subsection{Noise Augmentation as a Transfer Strategy}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-noise}

Having established noise augmentation as a form of domain randomisation (Section~\ref{sec:lit-sim2real-da}), the critical question becomes whether existing \gls{eit} noise models are adequate for this purpose. The evidence suggests they are not. \textcite{chen2023} add white Gaussian noise at a fixed \gls{snr} during CNN-TR training. \textcite{yao2022} augment with Gaussian noise and background (no-touch) samples. Neither considers contact impedance variation, electrode bias, or quantisation effects. Critically, \textcite{martin1995error} demonstrated that neural networks trained with noisy \gls{eit} data achieve substantially better performance under noisy conditions but at the cost of significant degradation on clean data---an order-of-magnitude robustness-versus-precision trade-off that no subsequent work has attempted to optimise through multi-component noise design. Furthermore, no prior work systematically investigates \emph{which} noise components are most critical for robust training.

This matters because the benefit of noise augmentation depends critically on the \emph{correspondence} between training-time augmentation and test-time corruption. \textcite{zheng2020noise} demonstrated in the image domain that augmenting with one corruption type provides limited robustness against others, and that mismatched augmentation can \emph{harm} clean-data performance without corresponding robustness gains. This necessitates physically-motivated noise model design rather than arbitrary augmentation. Quantifying this correspondence requires formal distributional divergence measures; \textcite{gretton2012kernel} developed the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) as a principled two-sample test in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, providing the metric adopted in the present work to measure how far each noise component shifts the data distribution from clean simulation.

\subsection{Limitations and Counter-Arguments for Noise Augmentation}
\label{sec:lit-sim2real-counterargs}

It is important to acknowledge conditions under which noise augmentation may fail, as these inform the design choices made here.

\paragraph{The precision--robustness trade-off.} \textcite{martin1995error} demonstrated that noise-trained networks degrade substantially on clean data. If the deployment environment is well-controlled (e.g.\ laboratory settings with frequent recalibration), noise augmentation may reduce overall performance without compensating benefit. The present work evaluates performance across a \emph{range} of severity levels, enabling practitioners to select augmentation intensity appropriate to deployment rather than assuming maximal noise is always beneficial.

\paragraph{Model mismatch.} Noise augmentation assumes the training-time noise distribution covers the test-time distribution. If a dominant real-world distortion is omitted or misspecified, augmented training provides only false robustness \parencite{zheng2020noise}. The present work mitigates this through four physically-motivated components derived from experimental literature, while acknowledging that unmeasured distortions (electromagnetic interference, non-stationary contact dynamics) remain outside the model's scope.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.} Domain adaptation methods \parencite{ganin2016, tzeng2017adda} offer a complementary strategy that learns domain-invariant representations directly from unlabelled target data, and may be superior when noise characteristics are unknown or too complex to model parametrically. This dissertation positions noise augmentation as a \emph{first step} that requires no real hardware data, with domain adaptation as a natural extension when real measurements become available.

\section{Deployment Considerations}
\label{sec:lit-deployment}

\subsection{Scalability and Power Constraints}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-scale}

Prosthetic skin systems must operate within tight power, weight, and computational budgets. \textcite{wang2020} demonstrated that adaptive measurement strategies can reduce energy consumption without sacrificing acceptable accuracy. \textcite{zhang2022fista} argued that classical algorithms (FISTA: $<$30 iterations) are more deployment-appropriate than deep learning for embedded systems. These constraints inform the present work's comparison of lightweight classifiers (\gls{svm}, \gls{rf}) against deeper CNN architectures, providing practical guidance on model selection for resource-constrained systems.

The scalability challenge extends beyond computation. \textcite{lee2020} noted that increasing sensor density leads to more wiring and reduced flexibility, while \textcite{jiang2023} highlighted that stretchable interconnects and geometrical designs limit sensor packing density. \gls{eit}'s ability to provide distributed sensing from few boundary electrodes directly addresses this scalability challenge \parencite{silvera-tawil2015}, though at the cost of ill-posed reconstruction.

\subsection{Long-term Reliability and Drift}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-drift}

Long-term operational stability is a prerequisite for prosthetic deployment, yet most studies evaluate only short-term performance. \textcite{kim2019} tested over 10,000 cycles and observed gradual degradation due to material fatigue and conductivity changes, confirming that sensor drift is unavoidable rather than merely a risk. \textcite{lee2019} showed that self-healing materials can restore functionality after damage, but the healing process itself may alter calibration. No study proposes or validates learning-based compensation for progressive drift. This is a significant gap given that recalibration is impractical for daily prosthetic use.

\subsection{Ethical and Societal Considerations}
\label{sec:lit-deployment-ethics}

The development of tactile-sensing prosthetics raises ethical considerations that the literature largely overlooks. The restoration of touch has documented psychological benefits for amputees \parencite{biddiss2007} but also raises questions about sensory overload and user consent for neural interfacing \parencite{chortos2016}. More pressingly, advanced prosthetic systems remain economically inaccessible to many of those who need them: \textcite{mcdonald2021} document that limb loss disproportionately affects low- and middle-income populations, meaning that high-cost sensing technologies risk widening healthcare inequalities rather than reducing them. The present work addresses accessibility indirectly by developing an open-source pipeline (EIDORS, PyTorch) that lowers the barrier to subsequent research and reproduction, though the broader ethical dimensions of prosthetic sensing technology deserve more systematic attention than they currently receive in the engineering literature.

Three further gaps---long-term drift compensation, inter-user variability, and multi-touch classification---are identified as important but beyond the scope of this dissertation, which focuses on the foundational question of noise robustness for single-contact classification.

\paragraph{A note on publication bias.} The overwhelmingly positive results reported in the reviewed literature (accuracies of 91--100\%) warrant scepticism. Publication bias favours positive outcomes; studies where noise models fail or where classifiers do not outperform baselines are unlikely to appear in the published record. This creates a distorted landscape where the difficulty of the deployment challenge may be substantially underestimated. The present work addresses this bias directly by reporting all experimental conditions and by providing effect sizes alongside $p$-values to distinguish statistical from practical significance.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} Taken together, the literature reveals a field that has demonstrated the \emph{feasibility} of ML-enabled EIT tactile sensing but has not yet established its \emph{reliability}. The gap between controlled laboratory demonstrations (98.7\% accuracy) and deployment requirements (robust operation under months of use with multiple noise sources) remains largely unquantified. This dissertation provides the first systematic quantification of that gap through a controlled simulation framework---including formal domain gap metrics \parencite{gretton2012kernel}, confidence calibration analysis \parencite{guo2017calibration}, per-class vulnerability profiling, and noise parameter sensitivity verification---establishing a baseline against which future hardware-validated work can measure progress. Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} details how these gaps are addressed through a controlled simulation framework with systematic noise augmentation and multi-model evaluation.
