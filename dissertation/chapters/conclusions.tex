\chapter{Conclusions}

\section{Summary of Key Findings}
\label{sec:conc-summary}

This dissertation investigated whether multi-component, physically-motivated noise augmentation improves classifier robustness for \gls{eit} tactile sensing, framing noise-matched training as domain randomisation for simulation-to-reality transfer. The principal findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The simulation-to-reality gap is severe and universal: all models collapse to chance level ($\approx$20\%) when trained on clean simulation data and evaluated under realistic multi-component noise, regardless of architecture or feature representation.
    
    \item Noise-matched training substantially bridges this gap: a 1D-CNN trained on noise-corrupted data achieves 76.1\% accuracy on noisy evaluation (+56.7pp over clean-trained), with graceful degradation to 94.5\% at 3$\times$ training severity.
    
    \item The standard Gaussian-only noise model assumed in prior \gls{eit} literature is insufficient to expose the real challenge: all models achieve $>$99\% under Gaussian noise alone, masking the vulnerability to electrode bias and contact impedance.
    
    \item Online noise augmentation (domain randomisation with per-epoch random noise) fails completely when the test-time noise is fixed---a fundamental insight about when domain randomisation strategies transfer to sensor modalities with fixed per-device characteristics.
    
    \item Electrode positioning bias dominates the domain gap (99.8\% of noise energy, MMD = 0.387), while contact impedance drives the fastest accuracy degradation under severity scaling. These provide actionable hardware design priorities.
    
    \item Models are catastrophically overconfident under domain shift (ECE = 0.795), identifying uncertainty quantification as a prerequisite for safety-critical prosthetic deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:conc-recommendations}

Based on the quantitative findings, the following evidence-grounded recommendations are offered:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{For \gls{eit} researchers}: adopt multi-component noise models (minimum: electrode bias + contact impedance) when reporting classifier accuracy. The Gaussian-only assumption produces $>$99\% accuracy that fundamentally mischaracterises deployment readiness; the 76.1\% under full noise reveals the true challenge magnitude.
    \item \textbf{For hardware designers}: prioritise electrode-substrate interface quality (contact impedance causes $-$1.9pp per severity unit) over measurement chain improvements (16-bit quantisation contributes 0.0pp degradation). Manufacturing tolerance on electrode placement (bias $\sigma$ = 0.01--0.05) should be the primary quality control metric.
    \item \textbf{For ML practitioners}: online augmentation that randomises noise per-epoch is counterproductive for sensor modalities with fixed per-device noise characteristics. However, simply fixing noise per training instance is also insufficient (19.9\% accuracy)---the training noise must be drawn from the \emph{same distributional instance} as the deployment environment. This necessitates device-specific calibration or domain adaptation rather than generic augmentation strategies.
    \item \textbf{For deployment}: implement explicit uncertainty quantification alongside any noise-trained classifier. The ECE reduction from 0.795 to 0.053 when distributions match confirms that calibration failure is a domain shift phenomenon---post-hoc calibration on target-domain data is essential.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

Five directions emerge naturally from these findings, ordered by expected impact:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Device-specific noise calibration.} The fixed-bias augmentation experiment (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) demonstrated that noise persistence alone is insufficient---the model requires exposure to the same distributional instance encountered at test time. Future work should explore few-shot calibration protocols where a small number of unlabelled measurements from the target device ($n \approx 10$--50) are used to estimate its specific electrode bias vector, enabling training-time injection of device-matched noise characteristics.
    
    \item \textbf{Domain-adversarial adaptation.} Apply DANN \parencite{ganin2016} or ADDA \parencite{tzeng2017adda} using a small set ($n \approx 50$--100) of unlabelled measurements from the target hardware, explicitly aligning feature distributions without requiring labelled real data---the standard approach for bridging the domain gap characterised by MMD = 0.384.
    
    \item \textbf{Hardware validation.} Deploy the trained CNN on a physical 16-electrode hydrogel sensor \parencite{yao2022} to measure the true sim-to-real gap and validate whether the noise model's assumptions (Gaussian bias distributions, independence across electrodes) hold empirically. The different-draw test (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) provides simulation-level evidence against memorisation, but hardware validation remains essential to confirm that the parametric noise model captures the dominant real-world degradation modes.
    
    \item \textbf{Three-dimensional forward modelling and adaptive meshing.} The mesh convergence study (Section~\ref{sec:method-mesh}, Table~\ref{tab:mesh-convergence}) established that the point-contact class is intrinsically sub-mesh-scale at all 2D refinements tractable for this dataset size, requiring a nearest-element fallback to produce class-consistent signals. A 3D forward model with locally adaptive meshing near the contact region---using, for example, EIDORS' \texttt{ng\_mk\_*} Netgen interface with a contact-conditional refinement criterion---would resolve focal contacts as genuine spatially-extended perturbations rather than discrete element-level approximations. This is also the natural setting in which to address the 2D$\to$3D approximation error identified in Section~\ref{sec:disc-approx-error}, which is the largest uncharacterised forward-model discrepancy. The principal cost is computational: each 3D forward solve takes orders of magnitude longer than the 2D equivalent, requiring either GPU-accelerated solvers or reduced-order modelling to remain tractable at the dataset size used here.
    
    \item \textbf{Temporal modelling.} Extend to sequential frames using recurrent or temporal convolutional architectures, enabling drift compensation and dynamic touch detection \parencite{silvera-tawil2012eit}. Temporal redundancy may improve noise robustness by averaging out frame-to-frame variation while preserving persistent signals.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reflections on the Research Process}
\label{sec:conc-reflections}

In retrospect, three methodological decisions would be revised if beginning this research anew. First, the fixed-bias augmentation experiment---ultimately conducted as a follow-up analysis (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation})---should have been included in the initial experimental design. While the result (19.9\% noisy accuracy) deepens rather than resolves the augmentation paradox, having this evidence from the outset would have motivated earlier investigation of device-specific calibration strategies. Second, the ablation study would benefit from the same 5-fold cross-validation protocol as the main experiment rather than the single-seed extended-training protocol adopted for computational convenience; this methodological inconsistency, while acknowledged and explained, slightly undermines confidence in the ablation findings. Third, earlier engagement with the MMD diagnostic---conducting domain gap analysis before rather than after the main experiments---would have informed the experimental design by revealing the dominance of electrode bias prior to model training, potentially motivating bias-specific mitigation strategies alongside the general noise augmentation approach. These reflections highlight the inherently iterative nature of empirical research, where findings from later analyses frequently suggest improvements to earlier design decisions.
