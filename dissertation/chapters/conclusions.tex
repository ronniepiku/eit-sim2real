\section{Summary of Key Findings}
\label{sec:conc-summary}

This dissertation investigated whether multi-component, physically-motivated noise augmentation improves classifier robustness for \gls{eit} tactile sensing, framing noise-matched training as domain randomisation for simulation-to-reality transfer. The principal findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The simulation-to-reality gap is severe and universal: all models collapse to chance level ($\approx$20\%) when trained on clean simulation data and evaluated under realistic multi-component noise, regardless of architecture or feature representation.
    
    \item Noise-matched training substantially bridges this gap: a 1D-CNN trained on noise-corrupted data achieves 76.1\% accuracy on noisy evaluation (+56.7pp over clean-trained), with graceful degradation to 94.5\% at 3$\times$ training severity.
    
    \item The standard Gaussian-only noise model assumed in prior \gls{eit} literature is insufficient to expose the real challenge: all models achieve $>$99\% under Gaussian noise alone, masking the vulnerability to electrode bias and contact impedance.
    
    \item Online noise augmentation (domain randomisation with per-epoch random noise) fails completely when the test-time noise is fixed---a fundamental insight about when domain randomisation strategies transfer to sensor modalities with fixed per-device characteristics.
    
    \item Electrode positioning bias contributes 99.8\% of total noise magnitude, while contact impedance drives the fastest accuracy degradation under severity scaling. These provide actionable hardware design priorities.
    
    \item Models are catastrophically overconfident under domain shift (\gls{ece} $= 0.795$), identifying uncertainty quantification as a prerequisite for safety-critical prosthetic deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:conc-recommendations}

Based on the quantitative findings, the following evidence-grounded recommendations are offered:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{For \gls{eit} researchers}: adopt multi-component noise models (minimum: electrode bias + contact impedance) when reporting classifier accuracy. The Gaussian-only assumption produces $>$99\% accuracy that fundamentally mischaracterises deployment readiness.
    \item \textbf{For hardware designers}: prioritise electrode--substrate interface quality over measurement-chain improvements. Manufacturing tolerance on electrode placement should be the primary quality-control metric; 16-bit ADC resolution is unnecessary for the noise regime studied.
    \item \textbf{For ML practitioners}: online augmentation that randomises noise per epoch is counterproductive for sensor modalities with fixed per-device characteristics. The training noise must preserve the within-frame correlation structure encountered at test time, which generic per-frame randomisation does not.
    \item \textbf{For deployment}: implement explicit uncertainty quantification alongside any noise-trained classifier. The ECE recovery from 0.795 to 0.053 when training and test distributions match shows that calibration failure is a domain-shift phenomenon, requiring post-hoc calibration on target-domain data.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

Five directions emerge naturally from these findings, ordered by expected impact:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Device-specific noise calibration.} Few-shot calibration protocols using a small number ($n \approx 10$--50) of unlabelled measurements from the target device to estimate its electrode-bias vector, enabling training-time injection of device-matched noise.
    
    \item \textbf{Domain-adversarial adaptation.} Apply DANN \parencite{ganin2016} or ADDA \parencite{tzeng2017adda} with a modest set of unlabelled target-hardware measurements to align feature distributions without requiring labelled real data.
    
    \item \textbf{Hardware validation.} Deploy the trained CNN on a physical 16-electrode hydrogel sensor \parencite{yao2022} to measure the true sim-to-real gap and test whether the parametric noise model captures the dominant real-world degradation modes.
    
    \item \textbf{Three-dimensional forward modelling.} A 3D forward model with locally adaptive meshing near the contact region would simultaneously address the 2D$\to$3D approximation error identified in Section~\ref{sec:disc-approx-error} and resolve the sub-mesh-scale point-contact class as a genuine spatially-extended perturbation. The principal cost is computational, motivating reduced-order or learned forward models.
    
    \item \textbf{Temporal modelling.} Extend to sequential frames using recurrent or temporal convolutional architectures, enabling drift compensation and dynamic touch detection \parencite{silvera-tawil2012eit}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reflections on the Research Process}
\label{sec:conc-reflections}

With hindsight, the ablation study would have benefited from the same five-seed stratified protocol as the main experiment rather than the single-seed extended-training protocol adopted for computational convenience; this methodological inconsistency is acknowledged in Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations} and slightly limits the statistical confidence of the ablation findings. More broadly, the iterative shape of the project---where the augmentation-paradox follow-up analyses (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) substantially refined the interpretation of the headline result---underscores the value of building investigative slack into the experimental design from the outset.
