\chapter{Conclusions}

\section{Summary of Key Findings}
\label{sec:conc-summary}

This dissertation investigated whether multi-component, physically-motivated noise augmentation improves classifier robustness for \gls{eit} tactile sensing, framing noise-matched training as domain randomisation for simulation-to-reality transfer. The principal simulation-backed findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The simulation-to-reality gap is severe and universal: all models collapse to chance level ($\approx$20\%) when trained on clean simulation data and evaluated under realistic multi-component noise, regardless of architecture or feature representation.
    
    \item Noise-matched training substantially bridges this gap: a 1D-CNN trained on noise-corrupted data achieves 75.7\% accuracy on noisy evaluation (+55.5pp over clean-trained), with graceful degradation to 93.3\% at 3$\times$ training severity.
    
    \item The standard Gaussian-only noise model assumed in prior \gls{eit} literature is insufficient to expose the real challenge: the clean-trained CNN retains $>$99\% accuracy under Gaussian noise alone, masking the vulnerability to electrode bias and contact impedance.
    
    \item Online noise augmentation (domain randomisation with per-epoch random noise) fails completely when the test-time noise is fixed---a fundamental insight about when domain randomisation strategies transfer to sensor modalities with fixed per-device characteristics.
    
    \item Electrode positioning bias dominates the domain gap (99.8\% of noise energy, MMD = 0.387), while contact impedance drives the fastest accuracy degradation under severity scaling. These provide actionable hardware design priorities.
    
    \item Models are catastrophically overconfident under domain shift (ECE = 0.796), identifying uncertainty quantification as a prerequisite for safety-critical prosthetic deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:conc-recommendations}

Based on the quantitative findings, the following evidence-grounded recommendations are offered:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{For \gls{eit} researchers}: report robustness with multi-component noise (at minimum electrode bias + contact impedance), and treat Gaussian-only results as incomplete robustness evidence.
    \item \textbf{For training strategy design}: use simultaneous multi-error training as the default benchmark pipeline, and keep component-wise training for mechanism-focused diagnostics. Apply Python-side synthetic noise in raw voltage space before re-scaling to preserve physical meaning.
    \item \textbf{For hardware designers}: prioritise electrode-substrate interface control over further ADC precision increases; use electrode-placement tolerance as a primary quality-control target.
    \item \textbf{For ML practitioners}: avoid per-epoch re-randomisation as the sole robustness strategy for fixed-device sensors; prefer persistence-aware augmentation and, where possible, device-specific calibration.
    \item \textbf{For deployment-oriented studies}: pair noise-trained classifiers with explicit uncertainty quantification and target-domain calibration before making deployment claims.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

Six directions emerge naturally from these findings, ordered by expected impact:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Foundational-vs-curriculum training comparison.} This work established simultaneous multi-error training as the strongest baseline for robustness, but did not exhaustively compare staged curricula (e.g., contact-impedance pretraining $\rightarrow$ electrode-bias adaptation $\rightarrow$ joint fine-tuning). A controlled study should quantify whether curriculum ordering offers optimisation benefits over direct foundational training while preserving joint-domain performance.

    \item \textbf{Device-specific noise calibration.} The fixed-bias augmentation experiment (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) demonstrated that per-device noise persistence---even with an independent noise realisation---recovers strong noisy-domain accuracy. Future work should explore few-shot calibration protocols where a small number of unlabelled measurements from the target device ($n \approx 10$--50) are used to estimate its specific electrode bias distribution, enabling training-time injection of device-matched, persistent noise characteristics.
    
    \item \textbf{Domain-adversarial adaptation.} Apply DANN \parencite{ganin2016} or ADDA \parencite{tzeng2017adda} using a small set ($n \approx 50$--100) of unlabelled measurements from the target hardware, explicitly aligning feature distributions without requiring labelled real data---the standard approach for bridging the domain gap characterised by MMD = 0.384.
    
    \item \textbf{Hardware validation across sensor morphologies.} Deploy the trained CNN on physical 16-electrode sensors to measure the true sim-to-real gap and validate whether the noise model's assumptions (Gaussian bias distributions, independence across electrodes) hold empirically. A particularly important comparison is conventional hydrogel versus silicone-encapsulated lattice hydrogel designs \parencite{yao2022, dong2025lattice}: this would quantify how much robustness gain comes from structural signal amplification versus foundational multi-error training. The different-draw test (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) provides simulation-level evidence against memorisation, but hardware validation remains essential to confirm that the parametric noise model captures the dominant real-world degradation modes.
    
    \item \textbf{Three-dimensional forward modelling and adaptive meshing.} The mesh convergence study (Section~\ref{sec:method-mesh}, Table~\ref{tab:mesh-convergence}) established that the point-contact class is intrinsically sub-mesh-scale at all 2D refinements tractable for this dataset size, requiring a nearest-element fallback to produce class-consistent signals. A 3D forward model with locally adaptive meshing near the contact region---using, for example, EIDORS' \texttt{ng\_mk\_*} Netgen interface with a contact-conditional refinement criterion---would resolve focal contacts as genuine spatially-extended perturbations rather than discrete element-level approximations. This is also the natural setting in which to address the 2D$\to$3D approximation error identified in Section~\ref{sec:disc-approx-error}, which is the largest uncharacterised forward-model discrepancy. The principal cost is computational: each 3D forward solve takes orders of magnitude longer than the 2D equivalent, requiring either GPU-accelerated solvers or reduced-order modelling to remain tractable at the dataset size used here.
    
    \item \textbf{Temporal modelling.} Extend to sequential frames using recurrent or temporal convolutional architectures, enabling drift compensation and dynamic touch detection \parencite{silvera-tawil2012eit}. Temporal redundancy may improve noise robustness by averaging out frame-to-frame variation while preserving persistent signals.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reflections on the Research Process}
\label{sec:conc-reflections}

In retrospect, three methodological decisions would be revised if beginning this research anew. First, the fixed-bias augmentation experiment---ultimately conducted as a follow-up analysis (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation})---should have been included in the initial experimental design. Because the result (69.5\% noisy accuracy) ultimately \emph{resolves} the augmentation paradox---showing that per-device noise persistence, not exact realisation match, is the critical factor---having this evidence from the outset would have sharpened the experimental framing and motivated earlier investigation of persistence-preserving augmentation strategies. Second, the ablation study would benefit from full protocol harmonisation with the main experiment (same condition matrix and reporting schema) so component-level findings and primary performance results can be compared without caveats about evaluation setup. Third, earlier engagement with the MMD diagnostic---conducting domain gap analysis before rather than after the main experiments---would have informed the experimental design by revealing the dominance of electrode bias prior to model training, potentially motivating bias-specific mitigation strategies alongside the general noise augmentation approach. These reflections highlight the inherently iterative nature of empirical research, where findings from later analyses frequently suggest improvements to earlier design decisions.
