\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

This chapter critically interprets the experimental findings in the context of the research objectives (Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}), prior literature (Chapter~\ref{ch:literature_review}), and deployment implications. The discussion is structured around four themes: the nature of the sim-to-real gap (\S\ref{sec:disc-gap}), the augmentation paradox (\S\ref{sec:disc-paradox}), class-level vulnerability analysis (\S\ref{sec:disc-class}), and deployment readiness (\S\ref{sec:disc-deployment}).

\section{Nature of the Simulation-to-Reality Gap}
\label{sec:disc-gap}

The universal collapse to chance level under Clean$\to$Noisy evaluation ($\approx$20\% across all architectures and feature spaces) provides the clearest evidence yet that the sim-to-real gap in \gls{eit} tactile sensing is not addressable through model capacity alone. This finding extends the corruption robustness results of \textcite{hendrycks2019}---who demonstrated up to 40\% degradation for image classifiers---to a more extreme regime where multi-component noise completely eliminates learned representations. The statistical confirmation ($p < 0.0001$, Cohen's $d = -135.9$) establishes this beyond reasonable doubt. The exceptionally large effect size reflects the complete distributional mismatch between clean and noisy feature spaces---a more extreme regime than the partial degradations typically studied in domain adaptation, where test-time data retains some overlap with the training distribution.

Critically, the domain gap analysis (MMD = 0.384) localises the problem to a single dominant component: electrode positioning bias contributes 99.8\% of total noise energy and dominates the MMD (0.387 of 0.384 total). This structural insight reframes the sim-to-real challenge: it is not fundamentally a random-noise problem but a \emph{systematic bias problem}. Each electrode acquires a fixed but unknown offset that is constant within its 13 measurements but varies between electrodes, producing block-structured corruption in the 208-D vector. This connects directly to the electrode modelling concerns raised by \textcite{vilhunen2002} and \textcite{kolehmainen1997}, who documented similar structured artefacts in reconstruction contexts but did not evaluate their impact on downstream classification tasks.

The CNN's superiority on noisy data (+18pp over the next-best model, RF at 57.9\%) can be attributed to its convolutional architecture: kernels of size 5 spanning adjacent measurements naturally learn to detect and compensate for the block-structured bias pattern, functioning as implicit per-electrode bias-subtraction filters. This architectural advantage is absent in fully-connected models (MLP) and kernel methods (SVM) which treat each of the 208 measurements independently without exploiting their cyclic structure. The finding supports the argument of \textcite{lecun2015} that inductive biases matching the data structure are essential for robust generalisation, and extends this principle from spatial images to 1D measurement sequences with periodic structure.

The Gaussian-only comparison provides perhaps the most important contextualisation for the broader \gls{eit} community: under the noise model assumed by \textcite{yao2022} and \textcite{chen2023}, \emph{all} models achieve $>$99\% accuracy regardless of training strategy. This means prior reported accuracies of 98--99\% under Gaussian noise \parencite{yao2022} do not demonstrate meaningful robustness---they merely confirm that additive Gaussian noise at typical SNR levels is trivially handled. Only the multi-component model exposes genuine vulnerability, motivating this work's contribution.

\section{The Augmentation Paradox}
\label{sec:disc-paradox}

The most counterintuitive finding is the complete failure of online noise augmentation (19.8\%) despite the success of fixed noise training (76.1\%). This contradicts the established domain randomisation literature \parencite{tobin2017}, where diverse augmentation during training typically improves target-domain generalisation, and more broadly conflicts with the data augmentation best practices surveyed by \textcite{shorten2019}. The explanation lies in the temporal structure of the dominant noise component and exposes a previously unidentified failure mode of domain randomisation.

Electrode bias is \emph{fixed per sample} in both the training dataset and test dataset (drawn once during data generation). When the CNN is trained on the pre-generated noisy dataset, it encounters \emph{consistent} bias patterns across epochs for each sample. Over training, it learns to associate specific bias patterns with their true labels---effectively learning a joint model of (signal + fixed bias) $\to$ label. At test time, the test set shares the same noise generation process, so the learned bias-compensation transfers. However, this also implies that the model has \emph{memorised} the specific bias distribution rather than learning a distribution-invariant representation---a distinction with profound implications for deployment on unseen hardware where the bias distribution may differ.

In contrast, online augmentation draws \emph{new random bias} at each epoch. The same clean sample receives different bias patterns across epochs, so the only \emph{consistent} information is the underlying clean signal. The model converges to a clean-signal extractor that achieves 98.5\% on clean data but cannot accommodate the persistent bias encountered at test time. Formally, if $\mathbf{x}_{\text{noisy}} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{clean}} + \mathbf{b}$ where $\mathbf{b}$ is fixed at test time but randomised during training, the model learns $f(\mathbf{x}) \approx f(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{\text{clean}} + \mathbf{b}]) = f(\mathbf{x}_{\text{clean}})$ because $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}] = \mathbf{0}$ by the zero-mean bias assumption.

This finding has significant theoretical implications. Domain randomisation \parencite{tobin2017} succeeds when the test-time domain is a \emph{sample} from the randomisation distribution---i.e., when noise at deployment is also random and i.i.d.\ per frame. But real \gls{eit} hardware has \emph{fixed} electrode characteristics that produce consistent per-session bias, violating this assumption. The fixed-bias augmentation experiment (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) confirms this analysis: sampling noise once per training sample and holding it fixed across epochs (mimicking per-device persistence) still fails at 19.9\%---demonstrating that persistence alone is insufficient. The critical requirement is not merely temporal consistency but \emph{distributional match} between training and deployment noise. The appropriate augmentation strategy must therefore either: (a) calibrate to the specific target device's noise characteristics, or (b) employ explicit domain adaptation \parencite{ganin2016, tzeng2017adda} using calibration data from the target device. This insight represents a refinement of the domain randomisation framework specific to sensor modalities with fixed per-device characteristics---a category that extends beyond \gls{eit} to strain gauges, capacitive arrays, and other transducer systems exhibiting manufacturing variation.

\section{Per-Class Vulnerability and Physical Interpretation}
\label{sec:disc-class}

The per-class robustness profiling reveals a physically interpretable vulnerability hierarchy. Light touch ($-$14.7pp at 3$\times$ severity) is most affected because its signal magnitude (L2 = 0.004) is only $\sim$2.4$\times$ the residual noise floor (L2 = 0.005 after bias removal). As noise severity increases, the signal-to-noise ratio approaches unity and the class boundary erodes. This connects to the detection threshold analysis of \textcite{boone1996}, who established a minimum conductivity contrast of $\sim$2\% for reliable EIT detection---light touch operates near this boundary by design (5--15\% $\Delta\sigma$).

The t-SNE visualisations confirm that light touch and point contact clusters overlap under noise, as do firm press and distributed contact. This pairing is physically motivated: classes with similar L2 norms but different spatial extent become indistinguishable when spatial information is corrupted. The model conflates \emph{magnitude} with \emph{extent}---a physically reasonable confusion given that both produce similar integral voltage perturbations, differing primarily in the spatial distribution of those perturbations across electrode pairs.

The Noisy$\to$Clean asymmetry (52.6\%) reveals a complementary failure mode: the noisy-trained CNN never encounters the zero-vector class 0 (no contact) without additive bias, and systematically misclassifies clean zero-vectors as light touch. This is not a model deficiency but a training distribution problem---addressable through mixed training with correct bias persistence, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc-paradox}.

\section{Deployment Readiness and Safety Implications}
\label{sec:disc-deployment}

Three findings have direct implications for prosthetic deployment:

\paragraph{Calibration failure.} The catastrophic overconfidence (ECE = 0.795) under domain shift means that raw softmax outputs cannot serve as uncertainty indicators in safety-critical applications \parencite{guo2017calibration}. A prosthetic skin reporting 99.6\% confidence while achieving 20\% accuracy would provide dangerously misleading feedback to both the user and any autonomous grasp controller. In medical device contexts, this constitutes a systematic hazard under IEC 62304 software lifecycle standards, where predictive confidence must be validated against operational accuracy. Post-hoc calibration methods (temperature scaling) or explicit uncertainty quantification (Monte Carlo dropout, deep ensembles) are prerequisite for deployment---an aspect entirely absent from prior \gls{eit} classification studies \parencite{yao2022, chen2023}. The ECE improvement from 0.795 to 0.053 when training and test distributions match further suggests that calibration failure is a \emph{domain shift phenomenon} rather than an architectural deficiency.

\paragraph{Noise model completeness.} The sensitivity analysis confirms that results generalise across physically plausible parameter ranges (accuracy $>$97.5\% for all parameters except extreme 20\,dB SNR). This suggests the noise model captures the dominant degradation modes robustly. However, temporal drift---excluded by design---remains unaddressed. \textcite{lee2019} documented progressive baseline shifts in hydrogel substrates over 24-hour periods, a sequential phenomenon requiring temporal models rather than per-frame augmentation.

\paragraph{Component prioritisation for hardware design.} The ablation identifies contact impedance as the fastest-degrading component under severity scaling ($-$4.5pp from 0$\times$ to 3$\times$), suggesting that electrode-substrate interface quality is the highest-priority hardware improvement. Quantisation (16-bit) is effectively invisible, confirming that standard ADC resolution is more than sufficient for \gls{eit} tactile sensing. This empirical prioritisation---electrode interface $>$ noise floor $>$ measurement chain $>$ digitisation---provides actionable guidance for hardware designers, absent from prior computational studies. The finding that contact impedance degrades performance multiplicatively (consistent with its effect on drive current magnitude) while Gaussian noise is merely additive further clarifies why the multi-component model is essential: the dominant degradation mechanism is non-additive and therefore cannot be characterised by SNR alone.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

Several limitations bound the generalisability of these findings and must be acknowledged transparently:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Matched noise distribution.} The 76.1\% Noisy$\to$Noisy result uses the same noise generation process for train and test data, raising the question of whether the CNN has learned genuine noise-invariant features or has merely memorised the statistical structure of the specific noise distribution. Since train and test noise are drawn from the same parametric family (Gaussian biases with known $\sigma$), the model may exploit distributional regularities that would not transfer to a different physical system. This result therefore represents an \emph{upper bound} for matched-distribution deployment; genuine sim-to-real transfer with unknown real noise would likely yield lower accuracy.
    
    Three observations provide empirical evidence against pure memorisation. First, the different noise draw test (Section~\ref{sec:res-memorisation}) directly evaluates the trained model on 10 independently-generated noisy test sets with entirely different random seeds---producing different electrode bias vectors, contact impedance factors, and Gaussian noise instances---and finds negligible accuracy change ($-0.6$pp, std = 0.7\%). This demonstrates that the model generalises across noise \emph{instances} rather than memorising specific bias patterns. Second, the 5-fold cross-validation protocol evaluates on held-out test samples whose specific noise realisations were never seen during training, yet performance remains consistent (std = 0.6\%). Third, the severity sweep demonstrates graceful degradation at noise levels \emph{exceeding} the training distribution (94.5\% at 3$\times$ severity), which would not occur if the model had merely overfit to the training-time noise statistics. Nevertheless, a definitive test---training on one noise distribution and evaluating on a distinct noise distribution with different \emph{parametric} statistics (e.g., different $\sigma$ for electrode bias)---remains necessary to fully characterise the model's transfer limits and is identified as important future work.
    
    \item \textbf{2D geometry.} The forward model uses a 2D cross-section; 3D geometries introduce additional sensitivity patterns, out-of-plane current flow, and electrode coupling effects not captured here \parencite{adler2006}. Extension to 3D would increase the feature space substantially ($>$1000 measurements) and may alter the relative impact of noise components.
    
    \item \textbf{Static single-frame classification.} Temporal correlations in sequential measurements, which could aid classification through pattern-over-time features, are not exploited \parencite{silvera-tawil2012eit}. Recurrent or attention-based architectures processing measurement sequences may achieve superior noise robustness by leveraging temporal redundancy.
    
    \item \textbf{No hardware validation.} All results are simulation-based; the noise model is literature-justified but not empirically calibrated against a specific physical system. The assumed Gaussian distribution for electrode bias is a modelling choice---real bias distributions may be multimodal or heavy-tailed.
    
    \item \textbf{Single-seed ablation protocol.} The ablation study uses a single random seed with extended training (200 epochs) for computational tractability. While the 5-fold cross-validated main experiment provides robust estimates, the ablation results should be interpreted as indicative rather than statistically confirmed.
\end{enumerate}

These limitations are inherent to the simulation-first research design chosen to isolate the noise augmentation effect under controlled conditions. They do not invalidate the core findings---which demonstrate the \emph{principle} of noise-matched training and identify electrode bias as the dominant gap component---but constrain the quantitative confidence with which specific accuracy figures can be projected onto real hardware deployment.
